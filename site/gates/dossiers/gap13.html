2.1 The precise open problem, stated the way the community states it

Every candidate quantum theory of gravity is required to answer two questions about a black hole, and to date no theory answers either from first principles in a realistic four-dimensional setting.

Question A (the counting problem). The semiclassical Bekenstein–Hawking entropy $\(S_{\rm BH}=\frac{A_H}{4G}\)$ is derived thermodynamically/geometrically (Euclidean saddle-point free energy, or the first law \(dM=T\,dS\) applied to the horizon area) without ever exhibiting the microscopic states being counted. The gate asks for the statistical mechanical origin: a Hilbert space \(\mathcal H_{\rm horizon}\) and a state count \(N(A)\) such that $\(\log N(A) = \frac{A}{4G} + (\text{computable subleading corrections}),\)$ derived "for the right reason" — i.e., not obtained by first assuming \(S=A/4G\) and then constructing an ensemble sized to match it. This is the black hole information/microstate problem in its sharpest form, open since Bekenstein (1973) and Hawking (1975) first wrote down the area law and the thermal spectrum.

Question B (the unitarity/Page-curve problem). If gravity is to be a genuinely unitary quantum theory, the von Neumann entropy of the outgoing Hawking radiation, \(S_{\rm rad}(t)\), computed on a fixed complete Cauchy slice as the hole evaporates, must rise while the hole is young (tracking Hawking's semiclassical calculation) and then turn over and fall back to zero once roughly half the entropy has been radiated away (the Page time, \(t_{\rm Page}\sim\) half the evaporation lifetime), returning to zero when the hole is gone. This is the Page curve (Page, 1993). A theory that cannot reproduce this turnover either destroys information (violates unitarity) or requires some other resolution (remnants, baby universes, firewalls) — all of which carry their own well-known pathologies. The gate asks the frozen 13D geometry to produce \(S_{\rm rad}(t)\) with the correct qualitative shape and, ideally, the correct Page time.

These two questions are logically separable — a theory could count microstates correctly without having a mechanism for how those states get imprinted in the outgoing radiation, or vice versa — and the dossier treats them as two separate open legs (§7 below), each routed to a single named upstream parent rather than left as an undifferentiated mystery.

2.2 History of the problem in brief

  • 1973 — Bekenstein first proposed, on generalized-second-law grounds, that a black hole must carry an entropy proportional to its horizon area, fixing the coefficient only up to an undetermined constant of order unity.
  • 1975 — Hawking computed the thermal spectrum of quantum fields on a black-hole background, fixing Bekenstein's undetermined constant to exactly \(1/4\) in Planck units via the surface-gravity/temperature relation, and in the same stroke exposed the information paradox: if the emission is exactly thermal, information about what fell in appears to be destroyed, in apparent conflict with unitary quantum mechanics.
  • 1977 — Gibbons–Hawking put the area law on a Euclidean path-integral footing: the saddle-point free energy of the Euclidean black-hole solution, differentiated appropriately, reproduces \(S=A/4G\) as a thermodynamic identity, with no reference to microstates. This is exactly the route the present closure walks (§3.2 below) — it proves the value rigorously but is silent, by construction, on what is being counted.
  • 1986 — Susskind–Uglum (and the broader induced-gravity literature) proposed that the \(1/G\) appearing in \(S=A/4G\) is literally the same \(1/G\) that appears as a UV-divergent counterterm renormalizing Newton's constant when one integrates out matter fields near the horizon — i.e., that black-hole entropy is entanglement entropy of matter fields across the horizon, order by order absorbed into the running of \(G\). This became, and remains, the most influential candidate mechanism linking entanglement to the area law.
  • 1993 — Page showed that if black hole evaporation is unitary, the entanglement entropy between the hole and its radiation must follow a specific curve — rising while the radiation is younger than about half the total evaporation lifetime, then falling back to zero — purely from Page's-theorem statistics of a randomly-scrambled pure state cut into two subsystems of very different size. This turned "is evaporation unitary?" into a sharp, checkable curve rather than a philosophical dispute.
  • 1996 — Strominger–Vafa produced the first genuine microstate count: for a special class of five-dimensional extremal supersymmetric black holes in string theory, the degeneracy of BPS D-brane bound states was computed independently of gravity and shown to reproduce \(\log N = A/4G_5\) exactly, including the numerical coefficient, via a controlled weak-coupling/strong-coupling extrapolation protected by supersymmetry. This is the existence proof that microstate counting is possible in principle — but it works only because supersymmetry, extremality, and (typically) a higher-dimensional/asymptotically-non-realistic setting conspire to make the count computable and protected against corrections. It has never been extended to a realistic non-extremal four-dimensional Schwarzschild-type black hole of the kind that astrophysics actually produces.
  • 1994 — Fursaev–Solodukhin and, more generally, Lewkowycz–Maldacena (2013) gave the modern "conical-defect / replica" derivation of horizon entropy: analytically continuing the number of sheets \(n\) in a Euclidean replica manifold with a conical deficit at the would-be horizon, and extracting entropy via \(S=(n\partial_n-1)I_n|_{n=1}\). This is a purely geometric/topological derivation of the coefficient \(1/4\) (again, not a microstate count) and is the second route the present closure walks (§3.2).
  • Casini–Huerta and Solodukhin sharpened exactly what the entanglement-entropy route can and cannot deliver: Casini–Huerta showed that in gauge theories the entanglement entropy across a boundary carries scheme/regulator ambiguities in its area-law coefficient (it is not a scheme-independent observable by itself), while Solodukhin's graviton-entanglement mechanism is a proposal, not a theorem, for how gravity itself might restore a fixed coefficient. Together these results are why "the area-law coefficient is just entanglement entropy of matter across the horizon, full stop" cannot be asserted as a clean theorem — the naive scheme-independent identification fails, and something more structural is required to pin the coefficient.
  • 2019–2020 — the island / replica-wormhole program (Penington; Almheiri–Engelhardt–Marolf–Maxfield; Almheiri–Mahajan–Maldacena–Zhao; Penington–Shenker–Stanford–Yang) finally produced the first computations of \(S_{\rm rad}(t)\) that reproduce the Page curve from a bulk gravitational path integral, via new saddle points ("islands") that appear in the generalized entropy formula at late times. This is widely regarded as the deepest post-Hawking progress on the information problem.
