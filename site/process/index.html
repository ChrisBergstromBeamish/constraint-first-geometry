The Process

Six pages, read in order, walk the whole program from first principles to current status: a thought experiment, the constraints it forces, the shape those constraints select, how that shape is tested, what the three main manuscripts do with it, and — at a narrative level only — the discipline used to attack each open question honestly. No page here claims a result; each one points to the pages that carry the actual claims and their status labels.

What this section is, and is not. This is the narrative layer — how the pieces fit together and why. The actual claims, numbers, and honest status labels live on The Tests, Foundational Constraints, and Gates. If this section and one of those ever seem to disagree, the claim pages control.

Read the six pages in order

1

The thought experiment

Start from Gauss's law and the plain fact that a field measured outside a closed surface does not care how the source is arranged inside it. Treat that invariance as a constraint on the deep structure of space, not a curiosity to explain away later.

2

Constraints → a derived shape

Add the rest of the consistency demands physics already imposes — frame-independence, entanglement's refusal to factor, countability, unit-consistency — and ask what geometry survives all of them at once. The result is a selected family of candidate shapes, not a shape derived from nothing.

3

Testing the shape

A candidate is worthless until it is checked against numbers nobody tuned it to hit. See two checks anyone can run by hand in minutes, and how every check on this site carries an explicit status — proof, equivalence demonstration, consistency check, or open question with a named falsifier.

4

GUT / Quantum / TOE → the Foundational Constraints

Three linked manuscripts — grand unification, the quantum-mechanical structure, and a scoped attempt at a fully unified account — each pressure-test the shape from a different direction and feed what they find back into a sharper statement of the Foundational Constraints.

5

The process we built to attack them

Doing this honestly at scale needed an actual process — a disciplined, repeatable way of attacking each open question and grading the result without letting anything get rounded up. Narrative only: what exists and why it is trustworthy, not the internal mechanics.

6

Closure paths

A high-level map of where an open question can actually end up — closed against a measured anchor, reduced to a named constraint, or left open with a stated path to close it — and why every one of those endpoints is reported honestly rather than dressed up as more than it is.
