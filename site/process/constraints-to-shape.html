2. Constraints → a shape

The thought experiment on the previous page produced one hard requirement: whatever the deep structure of space is, it must reproduce Gauss-law invariance for gravity and electromagnetism together, exactly, at every radius. Alone, that requirement narrows the field but does not come close to picking a single geometry. This page adds the rest of the consistency demands physics already imposes, and shows what survives when all of them are applied at once.

The full constraint set — Foundational Constraints

Four consistency requirements, taken together, do the narrowing:

Invariance

Physics cannot depend on the observer's frame or on an arbitrary choice of coordinates — the Gauss-law demand from the thought experiment generalizes into this.

Nonseparability

An entangled system's parts cannot be treated as independent pieces. Whatever the underlying geometry is, it has to support states that genuinely refuse to factor.

Finiteness

Quantities that physics treats as fundamentally countable — not infinitely divisible without limit — constrain how the geometry can be built up and measured.

Units-covariance

Scales and units have to stay consistent across the whole construction; nothing may quietly change meaning between one part of the geometry and another.

These four are described in full, with what each one does and does not pin down, on Foundational Constraints. This page only summarizes how they are used.

What running the constraints together produces

Run these requirements rigorously as a joint filter over candidate geometries, and the space of possibilities collapses a long way — not to one hand-picked answer, but to a selected family of shapes compatible with everything demanded of it simultaneously. The survivor this program has frozen and been testing against real physics is a specific higher-dimensional structure: the four familiar dimensions of space and time, carrying a compact internal geometry — the flag manifold K₆ = SU(3)/T², a 2-sphere, and a folded circle, thirteen dimensions in all — not a place one could visit, but a structure whose internal patterns are read out as the particles and forces observed in nature. The full construction is on The Shape.

The honest word is "selected," not "derived." The constraints narrow the candidate space; they do not uniquely force one shape out of nothing. Multiple internal factors were checked against the same requirements, and the reasoning for why the surviving factors are the clean carriers of the observed gauge structure — and where the account is a selection among live alternatives rather than a forced uniqueness proof — is on Foundational Constraints. Overclaiming "forced" where the honest status is "selected" is exactly the kind of upgrade-without-work this program refuses to make — its own over-claims, when caught, go on the public refuted-claims list on Residuals with the disproof stated.

Elimination, not preference: the rival that was built and broke

"Selected" does not mean "liked best." The internal color carrier was fixed by a real disproof: the simpler-looking rival CP² = SU(3)/U(2) was built end-to-end — honestly, all the way through — and breaks at the gauge gate, because its isotropy group is gauge-active. K₆ = SU(3)/T² survives for a stated structural reason: T² is the unique purely-abelian isotropy an SU(3) quotient allows. A preference can be argued with; a construction that breaks is over. The full elimination, rival failure included, is on The Shape (Sections 4.1 and 8) and in the dedicated minimal-shape article, MINIMAL_SHAPE.

The caveat, kept visible: the elimination runs over the enumerated shelf of SU(3)-carrier candidates; completeness over all conceivable carriers remains open — which is exactly why the honest word above stays "selected."

Worked examples: how constraints actually chose the shape

The callout above states the outcome; these two examples show the work. In each case the process starts with a shelf of candidate carriers, charges every hidden assumption, tests the survivor against the physics the register already demands, and freezes what is left without re-tuning it. The first example is a rival that looked cheaper and broke once the whole bill was charged. The second is the survivor that carried the register and was frozen once, then reused downstream unchanged.

The cheaper-looking rival broke when the register was charged

CP² = SU(3)/U(2) is the tempting move: it carries SU(3) exactly and is two dimensions cheaper than the flag manifold. It breaks by two independent, both-sourced routes. Route A (the coset). Its isotropy U(2) is non-abelian, hence gauge-active under the centralizer rule, so it forces either an extra unwanted SU(2)×U(1) — gauge recovery (Gate 2) fails — or it isotropy-locks weak and hypercharge into color, violating admissibility. The maximal torus T² is the unique purely-abelian SU(3) isotropy, so K₆ = SU(3)/T² is the unique clean SU(3) carrier. Route B (the full build). Built end-to-end as a complete model (Witten 1981, CP² × S² × S¹), it passes the gauge gate but fails chirality (Gate 4): on CP² the family count is a continuous bundle-moduli choice — "three by dial." The gate's demand is on the kind of number: a deformation-proof integer. Three by dial fails even when it equals three.

Full worked example — why CP² is eliminated at the gauge and chirality gates

1. Question

Why not choose a smaller, cheaper-looking internal shape than K₆? CP² = SU(3)/U(2) carries the strong-force symmetry SU(3) exactly and does it in only four internal dimensions — two fewer than the six-dimensional flag manifold. On raw dimension count it looks like the economical winner.

2. The hidden assumption

The tempting move hides one assumption: the simplest-looking geometry is automatically the best geometry. The process replaces it with a stricter test: the winning geometry is the shortest honest description that still carries the full required physics — gauge pattern, rank, carriers, chiral families, and no unpaid symmetry directions. Cheap is charged only after complete.

3. The candidate space

The enumerated shelf of SU(3)-carrier candidates includes CP² = SU(3)/U(2) (the cheaper four-dimensional carrier) and the flag manifold K₆ = SU(3)/T² (six-dimensional). The source names other rivals on the same shelf — flat tori, the six-sphere, CP³, SU(3)-less candidates, and higher Grassmannians — each eliminated for carrying the wrong symmetry algebra or the wrong family count. This example follows the closest rival, CP².

4. Constraints applied (in order)

The constraints are charged in sequence, capability before economy:

  1. Carrier — the shape must carry the observed Standard Model content.
  2. Gauge recovery (Gate 2) — the geometry must return the Standard Model gauge pattern with no extra summand.
  3. No free label — every symmetry direction the isotropy generates must be paid for; an unpaid one is a failure, not a feature.
  4. Chirality / no mirrors (Gate 4) — the family count must be a deformation-proof topological integer, not a tunable choice.
  5. Economy — only once capability is satisfied may description length decide between survivors.

The two routes are worth keeping separate because they charge CP² independently. Route A is architecture-neutral: it does not depend on the anti-fitting rule at all, only on the fact that a non-abelian isotropy inside color is gauge-active. Route B is the end-to-end verdict: build the whole model honestly — CP² × S² × S¹ — let it pass the gauge gate, and watch it fail chirality because the three families are a dial rather than a fixed integer.

6. Survivor / endpoint

The endpoint is not "CP² is impossible in any theory." It is narrower and honest: CP² is eliminated inside the declared candidate class because it fails the framework's gauge-recovery demand as a coset and fails the chirality demand as a full build. T² is the unique purely-abelian SU(3) isotropy, so K₆ = SU(3)/T² is the clean SU(3) carrier that survives. K₆ pays two extra dimensions for that forcedness — and buys a deformation-proof integer in return.

7. Measured anchors consumed

The elimination itself consumes no fitted number; it turns on the kind of quantity the family count is (topological integer vs. continuous dial). The relevant measured anchor enters only as a downstream consistency check: the LEP light-neutrino count Nν ≈ 2.984 ± 0.008 from the Z invisible width, consistent with exactly three families and no mirror partners. It is a check, not the lever — the count is forced by the geometry's index, not fitted to the measurement.

8. What was NOT claimed

Boundaries kept visible. This does not claim CP² is mathematically invalid, nor that it can never be useful in any theory — only that it fails this framework's declared carrier, gauge, and chirality demands. It does not claim the end-to-end CP² build fails the gauge gate: the source is explicit that it passes Gate 2 and fails Gate 4. And it does not claim K₆ is proven unique among every imaginable compactification — the elimination runs over the enumerated shelf of SU(3)-carrier candidates; completeness over all conceivable carriers remains open. The narrower, sourced statement — T² is the unique abelian SU(3) isotropy — is the one that is forced.

The survivor was frozen, not tuned

The carrier that survived is 4D spacetime × K₆ = SU(3)/T² × S² × the folded hypercharge circle S¹Y/ℤ₂ — thirteen dimensions in all. It is not tuned to fit results; it is frozen once, as the boxed active branch, and then reused unchanged. The spin-ℂ / Borel–Weil–Bott index on the frozen bundle is −3: three chiral families as a topological integer, with no dial to two or four. From that same unmodified shape the process reads the gauge multiset {su(3), su(2), u(1)} — 8 + 3 + 1 = 12 generators, rank 4, no extra summand — then hypercharge and anomaly closure, flavor, and proton safety, all downstream and all attached back to the one frozen object. The honest claim is not global uniqueness; it is that this was the leanest complete carrier left standing once every cost was charged.

Full worked example — why the 13D carrier was frozen once and reused

1. Question

Why did the process settle on exactly this thirteen-dimensional carrier — 4D spacetime × K₆ = SU(3)/T² × S² × S¹Y/ℤ₂ — and not on something with fewer visible pieces?

2. The hidden assumption

The assumption to charge is that a shape can be judged by raw simplicity before checking whether it carries the full physics. The corrected order is the opposite: a shape must first carry the register; only then may economy decide between the shapes that survive.

3. The candidate space

The survivor is built from named factors, each doing one job:

  • 4D spacetime — the four familiar dimensions.
  • K₆ = SU(3)/T² — the color / strong-force carrier (the flag manifold that beat CP² in the previous example).
  • S² — the weak-force carrier.
  • S¹Y/ℤ₂ — the folded hypercharge circle.

The rival carriers — CP², flat tori, the six-sphere, CP³, higher Grassmannians — were charged and eliminated on the same shelf before this product was frozen.

4. Constraints applied (in order)

  1. Carry the observed register. The shape must support the observed matter and force content.
  2. Return the force structure. Gauge symmetries must be read out of the geometry, not added as labels — the surviving multiset is {su(3), su(2), u(1)}, 8 + 3 + 1 = 12 generators, rank 4, no extra summand.
  3. Return the matter structure. Three chiral families and the charge routing must survive as topology, not as a tunable choice — the frozen-bundle index returns −3.
  4. Win by honest economy. Measured Standard Model content is held fixed on both sides; any hand-tuned quantity is charged. Only then does description length decide.

6. Survivor / endpoint

The endpoint label is derived-given-shape for what the carrier produces once the shape is granted, and the shape itself is selected / frozen inside the declared candidate family — the leanest complete carrier left standing after every cost was charged. K₆ "pays two extra dimensions for forcedness" (six versus CP²'s four) and buys the deformation-proof integer. That is the trade the process accepted deliberately.

7. Measured anchors consumed

The shape-selection step is spectrum-neutral: the observed Standard Model content is referenced as the register the shape must carry, not used as a tunable numeric input. Where measured numbers do enter downstream they enter as anchors and consistency checks — the light-neutrino count Nν ≈ 2.984 ± 0.008 (a check that three families is right, not the lever that forces it), and, in the flavor layer, the up-quark readout μu(MZ) ≈ 1.295 MeV against the measured 1.27 ± 0.43 MeV. The dimensionless factor that fixes that readout, 1/√6 = 1/√|S3|, is forced finite-group geometry (a one-chamber shadow of the Weyl group S3), not a fitted decimal — and it is a sharp falsifier: a tighter future measurement can kill this branch.

8. What was NOT claimed

Boundaries kept visible. This does not claim the thirteen dimensions were declared sacred at the start, nor that the shape was conjured with no prior candidate space and no charged assumptions. It does not claim global uniqueness — the carrier is selected inside the declared candidate family, not proven unique among every possible theory. And it does not claim the measured anchors were fitted: observed content is the register the shape must carry and a consistency check on what it returns, not a dial the shape was tuned against. The honest status is a selected, frozen branch that carries the register — reported inside an internal endpoint framework, not as external validation.
Read this as an audit, not a verdict from nature. These worked examples describe how candidates were charged and how the survivor was frozen inside an internal, endpoint-closed framework — it is not peer-reviewed and nothing here is experimentally confirmed as a theory of everything. Where a claim is forced it is labelled derived-given-shape; where a value comes from observation it is a measured anchor; where the survivor stands it stands as selected inside the declared candidate family, not proven unique among every possible theory. Open questions are named as open on Residuals.

Why bother with a shape at all

A geometry is only interesting if it earns its keep against measurement. The point of selecting a candidate shape is not aesthetic economy — it is that a single, fixed, higher-dimensional structure, once frozen, can be asked to reproduce the Standard Model's gauge group, its chirality structure, its family count, and a long list of flavor observables, all from the same unmodified geometry. That is a much harder thing to fake than fitting each number separately — and the frozen shape delivers it: exactly 12 gauge generators with nothing extra and nothing missing, exactly three families as a topological index, and 19+ flavor observables from two measured anchors, each itemized with its status on The Tests and Gates.
