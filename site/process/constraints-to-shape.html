2. Constraints → a derived shape

The thought experiment on the previous page produced one hard requirement: whatever the deep structure of space is, it must reproduce Gauss-law invariance for gravity and electromagnetism together, exactly, at every radius. Alone, that requirement narrows the field but does not come close to picking a single geometry. This page adds the rest of the consistency demands physics already imposes, and shows what survives when all of them are applied at once.

The full constraint set — Foundational Constraints

Four consistency requirements, taken together, do the narrowing:

Invariance

Physics cannot depend on the observer's frame or on an arbitrary choice of coordinates — the Gauss-law demand from the thought experiment generalizes into this.

Nonseparability

An entangled system's parts cannot be treated as independent pieces. Whatever the underlying geometry is, it has to support states that genuinely refuse to factor.

Finiteness

Quantities that physics treats as fundamentally countable — not infinitely divisible without limit — constrain how the geometry can be built up and measured.

Units-covariance

Scales and units have to stay consistent across the whole construction; nothing may quietly change meaning between one part of the geometry and another.

These four are described in full, with what each one does and does not pin down, on Foundational Constraints. This page only summarizes how they are used.

What running the constraints together produces

Run these requirements rigorously as a joint filter over candidate geometries, and the space of possibilities collapses a long way — not to one hand-picked answer, but to a selected family of shapes compatible with everything demanded of it simultaneously. The candidate this program has been testing against real physics is a specific higher-dimensional structure: the four familiar dimensions of space and time, carrying a compact internal geometry that is not a place one could visit, but a structure whose internal patterns are read out as the particles and forces observed in nature.

The honest word is "selected," not "derived." The constraints narrow the candidate space; they do not uniquely force one shape out of nothing. Multiple internal factors were checked against the same requirements, and the reasoning for why the surviving factors are the clean carriers of the observed gauge structure — and where the account is a selection among live alternatives rather than a forced uniqueness proof — is on Foundational Constraints. Overclaiming "forced" where the honest status is "selected" is exactly the kind of promotion this program tracks and refuses to make (see in the footer of every page).

Why bother with a shape at all

A geometry is only interesting if it earns its keep against measurement. The point of selecting a candidate shape is not aesthetic economy — it is that a single, fixed, higher-dimensional structure, once frozen, can be asked to reproduce the Standard Model's gauge group, its chirality structure, its family count, and a long list of flavor observables, all from the same unmodified geometry. That is a much harder thing to fake than fitting each number separately. The next page shows how that check is actually run.
