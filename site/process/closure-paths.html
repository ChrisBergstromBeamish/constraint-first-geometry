6. Closure paths

Every open question this program attacks has to end somewhere. This page maps, at a high level, the honest endpoints an attack can actually reach — and just as importantly, what does not count as an endpoint here. None of these is "solved" in the sense of a from-nothing proof; every one of them is an honest, named place for an answer to land.

The honest endpoints

Closed against a measured anchor

The question reduces to one of the small set of measured quantities this program already relies on elsewhere (for example, the Planck mass, a gauge coupling, the top Yukawa, a mixing angle). This is not a claim that the anchor itself was derived from nothing — it is a claim that the open question has been traced down to a number already in honest use.

Reduced to a Foundational Constraint

The question turns out to be a consequence of one of the four constraints — Invariance, Nonseparability, Finiteness, or Units-covariance — rather than an independent assumption. Reducing to a shared constraint already used elsewhere is treated as the strong outcome, not a weaker fallback.

Left open, with a named path

The honest current answer is "not yet resolved." A real open question on this site always carries a stated path forward — what computation, measurement, or argument would close it — rather than being left as a bare unknown.

Refuted

An attack sometimes shows a candidate answer, or an assumption behind it, is simply wrong. That outcome is kept on the public record rather than dropped, because a refutation sharpens the constraints for everything downstream of it.

What does not count as closure

Two moves are explicitly disallowed, because they are the most common ways a program like this can quietly deceive itself or a reader:

  • No claiming something was derived from nothing. A dimensionful or contingent quantity that isn't itself one of the measured anchors always has to bottom out somewhere — on a constraint or a measured input — never on thin air. A page that seems to get a number "for free" is a page that has not finished tracing its own reasoning.
  • No forcing a result down to a floor it did not earn. An open question does not get relabeled "resolved" just because it would be convenient. If the honest status is "open," it is published as open, and the count of anything promoted without new work is the public PROMOTIONS:0 stamp on every page.

Why this matters more than any single result

A theory that only reports its wins is not checkable. A theory that reports every claim with an honest, load-bearing status — including the ones that are not yet closed — gives a reader something real to push against. That is the entire bet this program is making: that being this specific about what has and has not been shown is worth more, to a serious reader, than a cleaner-looking but less honest scorecard.
