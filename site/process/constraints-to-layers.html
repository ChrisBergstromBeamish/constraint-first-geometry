From constraints to layers

A constraint is only useful if it can be enforced. That is why this candidate framework turns each of its three deep roots into layers. Shape is not just "geometry" — it has an object identity, a frozen branch identity, and a source fingerprint. Scale is not just "a number" — it has a magnitude type, a scheme and comparison window, and a tolerance rule. Granularity is not just "finite" — it has a candidate class, a finite record, and a reproducer check. Layers are not decoration. They are the machinery that stops a claim from quietly sliding between meanings between one gate and the next.

Why layers exist

Every root has a soft, everyday reading that a theory can lean on without noticing. "The geometry gives it," "the number matches," "it is finite so it is fine" — each of those is a place where a result can be smuggled in after the fact. Layers close those gaps by forcing each root to say exactly which object, which scale, and which finite record it is standing on. Concretely, layering stops three failures that otherwise look like success.

Object drift

Changing the object after a result is known. If "the shape" can be re-read as a slightly different manifold, bundle, or actor once the target number is in view, any near-miss can be nudged onto the answer. Shape layers pin the abstract object, the frozen branch, and a source fingerprint before the comparison, so the actor that produced a number is the same actor that was declared.

Scale drift

Comparing values in the wrong units, scheme, or window. A 13-dimensional ladder value is not a 4D running mass; a Planck-unit ratio is not a low-energy magnitude. Scale layers require the magnitude type, the scheme and comparison window, and the tolerance to be named, so nothing is judged against a ruler it was never built to meet.

Record drift

Treating a mathematical label as physical without a finite record. An exact zero-size point or an unpaid infinity can hide inside a "solution" and be read as real. Granularity layers demand a finite candidate class, a finite record or ledger, and a reproducible certificate — so a label only counts as physics if something finite actually records it.

Layers are enforcement, not style

The point of layering is that every gate in this candidate framework must state, out loud, what object it uses, what scale it compares at, and what finite record verifies it. A root with all three layers filled in can be audited by a stranger; a root left as a single vague word cannot. That is the whole difference between a claim that can be checked and one that only sounds checked.

Shape layers

Shape layers keep the object fixed. Before any number is compared, the shape has to answer three questions, and the answers are frozen so they cannot be revised once the target is in view.

1

What is the object?

The abstract identity. For this framework the frozen internal carrier is the flag manifold K₆ = SU(3)/T², together with a 2-sphere and a folded circle over four-dimensional spacetime — 4D × K₆ × S² × S¹Y/Z2, thirteen dimensions in all. The object is named as a specific structure, not as "the geometry."

2

Which branch or version is frozen?

The exact active branch. The carrier is frozen once as the boxed active branch and re-used downstream unchanged — the same shape carries the gauge recovery, the family count, the hypercharge and anomaly ledgers, the flavor readouts, and proton safety. There is no second, differently-tuned copy for a later gate.

3

What source or executable record proves the same object was used?

A fingerprint. Frozen ledger rows and a reproducible source artifact stand behind the object, so a reader can confirm that the shape which produced a number is byte-for-byte the shape that was declared, with no post-target modification.

Scale layers

Scale layers keep the comparison honest about units. Before a value is called a match, scale has to answer three questions.

1

Is this dimensionless or dimensionful?

The magnitude type. A dimensionless structural ratio, an absolute measured magnitude, a running mass at a stated energy, a cosmological density, and a scale-free curvature invariant are five different kinds of quantity and cannot be compared as if they were one.

2

What scheme, normalization, and window apply?

The comparison context. Running masses and couplings are compared at MZ; a Λ ratio is stated in Planck units; the electroweak normalization runs through vEW; a projected 13D value is only comparable after its declared 13D-to-4D readout map. The window is named before the value is judged.

3

What tolerance or uncertainty defines success?

The predicate. Success is spelled out as a measured uncertainty band, a σ pull, an exact structural equality, a consistency check only, or a measured anchor taken from observation — never an unstated "close enough."

Granularity layers

Granularity layers keep labels from posing as physics. It is a frame-independent floor on action, information, and record cost — not a smallest length, not a preferred frame, not a spacetime lattice. Before a mathematical feature is treated as real, granularity has to answer three questions.

1

What is the finite candidate class?

The admissible set. Only configurations that can be recorded at finite cost are allowed in; idealizations that require an exact continuum limit are excluded from the candidate class.

2

What labels are forbidden unless paid for?

No unpaid labels. A quantity written as exactly zero size, or an infinity carried along for convenience, is not automatically physical. If a label is to count, a finite record or ledger has to pay for it.

3

What ledger or reproducer records the check?

A certificate. A reproducible argument shows where the offending limit entered, why it violates the finite-cost floor, and what finite structure remains — and it keeps that check separate from neighboring questions it does not settle.

Worked examples

One worked example per root, each showing the same discipline: name the object, the scale, or the record; apply the constraints in order; and read off the honest endpoint. Open a card for the full walk-through.

Shape — freeze the actor before the number counts

The up-quark correction only became admissible after the K₆ chamber, the S3 Weyl group, the two-step A₂ actor, and the one-chamber shadow were named as the object being read. Without those layers, the 1/√6 factor would look like a patch; with them it is an auditable readout rule.

Full worked example — the actor had to be frozen before the number could count

Question

The candidate framework places the up quark on a rung of a 13-dimensional ladder. The question is narrow and sharp: does the frozen shape read out the measured up-quark mass, μu(MZ) ≈ 1.27 ± 0.43 MeV, without any free coefficient inserted after the fact?

The hidden assumption

The tempting move is to compare the raw 13D ladder value directly to a 4D running mass. That silently assumes the two are the same kind of quantity. They are not: the raw ladder value lives in the internal geometry, and the running mass lives in the 4D theory at MZ. Comparing them directly would let any correction factor be reverse-engineered to close the gap — the classic object-drift and scale-drift failures at once.

The candidate space

What is allowed to fix the readout is not "any decimal that works." The candidate correction has to come from the finite structure already named in the frozen shape: the K₆ = SU(3)/T² flavor geometry, the A₂ chamber plane, the S3 Weyl chambers, and the F⁺ flavor chamber. The one-chamber 4D shadow of that finite Weyl structure is what supplies a coefficient — a small, discrete menu, not a continuous dial.

Constraints applied (in order)

The Shape layers are filled in first, then the readout is taken:

1

No actor drift

The particle actor is fixed before the target is known: the up rung a = 2, the two-step oriented A₂ actor, the Weyl-alternating action, and the canonical one-chamber 4D shadow are all named as the frozen branch.

2

No free coefficient

The correction coefficient cannot be reverse-engineered; it must fall out of the declared structure.

3

Finite-group constraint

The coefficient must come from the finite S3 structure — a one-chamber shadow of the finite Weyl group S3, which has |S3| = 6 elements.

4

Readout constraint

The 13D ladder is compared to a 4D running mass only after the 13D-to-4D transport is declared, and the comparison is made at MZ.

Elimination ledger

Candidate readoutFate
Raw 13D ladder value compared directlyRejected — wrong-ruler comparison; a 13D value is not a 4D running mass.
A fitted decimal chosen to hit 1.27 MeVRejected — free coefficient, reverse-engineered after the target.
The forced 1/√6 = 1/√|S3| one-chamber shadowSurvives — forced finite-group geometry, declared before the comparison.

Survivor / endpoint

With the actor frozen and the transport declared, the corrected readout uses the forced dimensionless factor 1/√6 = 1/√|S3|, giving μu(MZ) ≈ 1.295 MeV against the measured 1.27 ± 0.43 MeV. The honest label is derived-given-shape: given the frozen shape, the coefficient is forced finite-group geometry, not a fitted decimal. It is endpoint-closed inside the framework, not experimentally confirmed.

Measured anchors consumed

One: the measured up-quark running mass μu(MZ) = 1.27 ± 0.43 MeV, used as the comparison target and falsifier — not as an input the coefficient was fitted to.

What was NOT claimed

This is a sharp falsifier, not a license to keep adjusting. A tighter future measurement of the up-quark mass can kill this branch. The layers are exactly what make that possible: because the actor and the coefficient were frozen in advance, there is no room to re-read the shape if the number moves.

Scale — a measured ruler stays a measured ruler

The Planck and electroweak scales are declared as measured rulers, and the hierarchy is simply their ratio — not a third, from-nothing number. The same discipline holds Λ as measured, with a failed alternative route serving as a check that the geometry is not secretly producing it.

Full worked example — a measured ruler stays a measured ruler

Question

Two questions of the same kind: why is the electroweak scale so far below the Planck scale — the hierarchy H = vEW / MPl ≈ 2×10−17 — and why is the cosmological constant Λ the tiny value it is? The scale layers decide, first, whether these are even the right questions to ask of a geometry.

The hidden assumption

The trap is to treat a measured magnitude as if it were pure geometry waiting to be derived. If vEW, MPl, or Λ can be "predicted from the shape," a value that was in fact read off an experiment gets laundered into a from-nothing success. Scale layers forbid that laundering by typing each quantity before anyone tries to derive it.

The candidate space

Before shape decides anything, shape does supply real structure at the electroweak scale: the charge rule Q = T3 + Y, exactly one massless photon, and the W/Z structure — confronted by the measured ρ0 = 1.00038 ± 0.00020, which is a test, not a fit. But the magnitudes of the scales themselves are a different kind of object. The candidate answers to "why this magnitude?" are: (a) a genuine from-nothing derivation, or (b) a measured anchor plus arithmetic. Layer 1 forces the choice by typing the quantity.

Constraints applied (in order)

1

Type the quantity

Do not compare dimensionful values as if they were pure geometry. MPl, vEW, and Λ are absolute measured magnitudes; H is a dimensionless ratio.

2

Declare the ruler

Where an absolute scale is needed, name the measured anchor: MPl is measured ruler #1; vEW ≈ 246 GeV is measured ruler #2.

3

Declare the window

State the scheme: MZ for running masses and couplings, Planck units for the Λ ratio, the electroweak normalization for vEW, and the cosmological extraction method for Λ.

4

Declare the predicate

Is the comparison exact, within an uncertainty band, a consistency check only, or a measured anchor? For the scales it is: measured anchor.

5

Reject laundering

Do not call a measured input a prediction.

Elimination ledger

Claim about a scaleFate
Derive vEW or MPl from the geometryRejected — both are measured rulers; deriving them from nothing would be laundering.
H = vEW / MPl as a third derived numberRejected — H is the arithmetic ratio of two measured rulers, not a separate derivation.
Λ predicted by an internal dark-energy dialRejected — the geometry has no such dial; Λ is measured.

Survivor / endpoint

Hierarchy. With both rulers declared measured, H ≈ 2×10−17 is exactly their ratio. The "why the hierarchy number?" demand is answered as a measured-anchor discipline — it does not derive vEW from MPl.

Λ. Λ ≈ (2.3 meV)4 ≈ 10−122 MPl4 is accepted as a measured anchor. The geometry has no internal dark-energy dial. A discreteness route that tried to produce it misses by about 113 orders of magnitude — and that miss is used as a negative control: it shows the geometry is not secretly generating the observed number by some hidden path.

Measured anchors consumed

Three, all taken from observation, none derived: MPl (Planck ruler), vEW ≈ 246 GeV (electroweak ruler), and Λ ≈ (2.3 meV)4. The measured ρ0 = 1.00038 ± 0.00020 is consumed only as a consistency test of the W/Z structure.

What was NOT claimed

No from-nothing prediction of vEW, MPl, or Λ. The hierarchy is not overclaimed as a derivation; it is honestly a ratio of measured rulers. Where an absolute scale was needed, a measured anchor was named and labeled as such.

Granularity — the zero-size point is not a record

The black-hole singularity appears only when the calculation is allowed to shrink to an exact zero-size point. Granularity blocks that step: a zero-size endpoint is not a finite physical record. The horizon stays real, the exterior stays Schwarzschild, and only the impossible infinite-density point dissolves.

Full worked example — the zero-size point is not a physical record

Question

Classical general relativity predicts an infinite-density, zero-size point at the center of a black hole. Is that singularity a real physical feature the framework must reproduce, or an idealization that fails the granularity floor?

The hidden assumption

The singularity is produced by letting the radial coordinate run all the way to r → 0. That step quietly assumes an exact zero-size limit is a legitimate physical endpoint. Granularity's finite-record layer denies exactly that assumption: an exact zero-size limit is not a finite physical record, so it cannot be an admissible endpoint.

The candidate space

The admissible candidates for the interior are the ones that can be recorded at finite cost: a finite-core black-hole interior with the standard exterior retained. The zero-size point is excluded from the class as a non-recordable limit. Crucially, this does not introduce a smallest length — the floor is on Lorentz-invariant action and information cost, not on distance.

Constraints applied (in order)

1

Identify the impossible distinction

The point singularity requires exact zero size.

2

Apply the finite-record constraint

Exact zero size is not a finite physical record, so it is not an admissible candidate.

3

Preserve finite observables

The horizon threshold and the exterior Schwarzschild solution remain real; no solar-system observable is shifted.

4

Do not overclaim

Entropy, information, and the Page curve are a separate question and are left untouched here.

Elimination ledger

Feature under testFate
Exact zero-size, infinite-density pointDissolves — not a finite physical record; a wrong-target continuum idealization.
Event horizonRetained — a finite, recordable threshold; stays real.
Exterior Schwarzschild geometryRetained — unchanged outside the core; no observable shifted.
Finite-core interiorReplaces the point — a finite, recordable interior.

Survivor / endpoint

The singularity is dissolved as a wrong-target continuum idealization: the original "what is at the zero-size point?" is shown to be a question about a non-recordable limit, not a real physical endpoint. The black hole does not vanish — the horizon remains physical, the exterior remains the standard Schwarzschild solution, and a finite core replaces the point.

Measured anchors consumed

None. This is a structural dissolution: it removes an idealization that no measurement ever recorded, while leaving every finite, observable feature exactly where it was.

What was NOT claimed

This does not claim to solve black-hole information, entropy, or microstate counting — the Page-curve and information questions are a separate gate and are not settled here. Granularity is not a smallest length, not a preferred frame, and not a spacetime lattice. Only the impossible zero-size point dissolves.
