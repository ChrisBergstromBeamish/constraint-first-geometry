From constraints to scale

A theory can have a beautiful shape and still fail physics if it cannot say how large, how strong, how heavy, or how energetic anything is. Scale is the rule that connects structure to measurement. In this candidate framework, scale is handled honestly: dimensionless ratios and patterns are where the geometry can do real work, while absolute rulers — the Planck scale, the electroweak scale, the observed value of Λ — are charged openly as measured anchors unless the framework actually derives them.

The problem

Many theories overclaim by turning measured values into hidden inputs and then calling them predictions. A magnitude gets quietly fixed to match the data, and the fit is re-narrated as a triumph. This page describes the opposite discipline: every measured value is charged in the open, so a genuine dimensionless prediction can be told apart from a laundered constant.

The values most easily laundered are the scale-sensitive ones — the quantities that carry a unit and therefore need a ruler:

  • the Planck scale (the gravitational ruler), MPl;
  • the electroweak scale, vEW ≈ 246 GeV;
  • the gauge couplings evaluated at the Z mass, αi(MZ);
  • absolute quark, lepton, and neutrino mass normalizations;
  • the cosmological constant, Λ;
  • reheating-scale inputs used for relic abundance and baryogenesis;
  • the comparison windows themselves — MZ and the low-energy observables a prediction is read against.

Every one of these is scale-sensitive. Left undisciplined, any of them can be slipped in as an input and read back out as if it had been foretold. The point of scale discipline is to make that impossible to hide.

The hidden assumption

The hidden assumption was that a beautiful structure should be able to produce every absolute number. But massive physics always needs at least one ruler.

The constraints that force scale discipline

Five constraints keep magnitudes honest. Applied in order, they separate what the geometry can legitimately output from what has to be read off the world.

1

Dimensionless-first

Only dimensionless claims — pure ratios and patterns — are candidate structural outputs. If a claimed result carries a unit, it is an absolute magnitude and cannot be a bare consequence of shape alone.

2

Measured ruler

Any physics with massive particles needs at least one absolute scale. That scale is taken from observation and labelled a measured anchor — not smuggled in as a derivation.

3

Scheme and window

Every comparison to data must declare its scale window, unit system, and normalization scheme. A number read at one energy is not the same number read at another; the window (for example MZ) is part of the claim.

4

No laundering

A measured value cannot be renamed as a prediction. Once a quantity is charged as an anchor, it stays an anchor everywhere it is used. This is the constraint that stops a fit from being re-narrated as a forecast.

5

Bridge

A 13-dimensional result cannot be compared to a 4-dimensional running mass without a declared transport and readout coefficient. The bridge from the internal geometry to a lab observable must be written down, not assumed to be the identity.

What scale does — and does not do

With these constraints in force, scale earns its keep. It closes or reframes questions by:

  • identifying which values are measured anchors, so they are visible rather than hidden;
  • turning hierarchy questions into ratios of measured rulers instead of new mysteries;
  • preventing wrong-ruler comparisons, where a raw internal number is held against the wrong observable;
  • letting genuine dimensionless predictions stand out, because the measured inputs are no longer in disguise.

What scale does not do is make measured values disappear. It tells the reader exactly where measurement enters — and that honesty is the whole point.

A measured anchor is not a failure; hiding one is. Scale does not conjure absolute magnitudes out of geometry. It marks the exact places where measurement enters, so a real prediction cannot be confused with a value that was quietly read off the world.

Worked example: shape gives the pattern, scale gives the ruler

The geometry gave the pattern; measurement gave the size

In the electroweak gate the shape gives the charge rule Q = T3 + Y, exactly one massless photon, and the W/Z structure. But the weak scale itself is charged honestly as a measured ruler. The hierarchy then becomes the ratio between two measured rulers — not a third mystery smuggled in as a prediction.

Full worked example — the electroweak hierarchy as a ratio of measured rulers

1. Question

How did the constraints produce both a geometric shape and an honest scale discipline — and what, exactly, is the electroweak hierarchy H = vEW / MPl ≈ 2×10−17 a statement about?

2. The hidden assumption

The tempting assumption is that a theory deep enough should derive every absolute number, so the tiny ratio between the weak scale and the Planck scale ought to fall out of the geometry as a prediction. The framework rejects that. The corrected assumption is: a theory with massive particles needs at least one measured ruler, and the honest task is to separate structural outputs from measured anchors rather than pretend the anchors were derived.

3. The candidate space

On the shape side, the frozen 13-dimensional carrier — 4D spacetime × K₆ = SU(3)/T² × S² × S¹Y/Z₂ — supplies the gauge structure, with the Higgs winding on an internal cycle supplying the electroweak embedding. On the scale side, the candidate magnitudes in play are the two absolute rulers (the Planck scale and the electroweak scale) plus the gauge couplings, the top Yukawa, and |Vus| wherever those are used as anchors. The question is which of these the geometry can output and which must be read from the world.

4. Constraints applied (in order)

  1. Object. Before comparing any numbers, freeze the object being measured — here the electroweak sector of the frozen 13D geometry.
  2. Dimensionless-first. Ask of each claim whether it is a pure ratio or pattern, or an absolute magnitude. The charge rule Q = T3 + Y, the single massless photon, and the W/Z structure are structural (dimensionless) outputs. The weak scale vEW carries a unit and is not.
  3. Ruler. Absolute magnitudes need measured anchors. Both vEW and MPl are absolute scales, so both are charged as measured rulers.
  4. Scheme and window. The comparison is declared at its window — the couplings and masses used are those at MZ.
  5. No laundering. vEW and MPl stay measured everywhere they appear. Their ratio is therefore arithmetic, not a new derived quantity.

6. Survivor / endpoint

The shape delivers the electroweak charge structure as a dimensionless, derived-given-shape result. The two absolute scales survive as measured anchors. The hierarchy H = vEW / MPl ≈ 2×10−17 is then the arithmetic ratio of two measured rulers — endpoint-closed inside the framework as a ratio, not as a newly derived magnitude. The structure side is confronted, not fitted: the W/Z sector meets ρ0 = 1.00038 ± 0.00020 as a test.

7. Measured anchors consumed

  • MPl — the measured Planck ruler (#1).
  • vEW ≈ 246 GeV — the measured electroweak ruler (#2).
  • ρ0 = 1.00038 ± 0.00020 — a confronted test value, not a fit target.

8. What was NOT claimed

vEW is not derived from nothing. MPl is not derived from nothing. The hierarchy is not predicted as an independent new number — it is the ratio of two anchors already charged. And no absolute mass is claimed to be predicted outright by shape alone.

Smaller cards from the same discipline

Up-quark: the wrong-ruler correction

The raw 13D ladder value for the up quark could not be compared directly to a 4D running mass — that is a wrong-ruler comparison. The bridge constraint forces a transport-and-readout coefficient: the finite-group factor 1/√6 = 1/√|S3|, a one-chamber shadow of the finite Weyl group S3. With it, the readout gives μu(MZ) ≈ 1.295 MeV against a measured 1.27 ± 0.43 MeV. The 1/√6 is forced finite-group geometry, not a fitted decimal — and it is a sharp falsifier: a tighter future measurement can kill this branch.

Λ: measured, with a negative control

The cosmological constant Λ ≈ (2.3 meV)4 ≈ 10−122 MPl4 is a measured anchor, not a prediction; the geometry carries no internal dark-energy dial. A failed discreteness route misses the observed value by roughly 113 orders of magnitude — and that miss is a negative control, evidence that the geometry is not secretly manufacturing the observed number.

The rule in one line

Dimensionless structure can be predicted or compressed by geometry; absolute size must be anchored to measurement unless a source derivation is actually supplied. Shape gives the pattern; scale gives the ruler.
