One but Separate

There is exactly one object in this framework — a single frozen 13-dimensional shape, fixed once, fingerprinted, and never re-tuned, that already carries the forces, the matter, and gravity. And yet the world arrives as many things: this particle and not that one, a detector apart from what it detects, you apart from the room you are standing in. This section states the framework's answer plainly: separateness is not fundamental. It is a record. A distinction is physically real exactly where a finite record has paid for it — and the record budget itself has a history: unpayable at the Big Bang, cheapest today. One object; earned separations. One, but separate.

The mechanism, stated plainly

Separation is earned and emergent because granularity is time-dependent — largest at the Big Bang, shrinking to its minimum at the present moment. At the Big Bang the cost of recording anything was unpayable: no distinction could be paid for, wave functions overlapped completely, and only “one” thing could be seen — the “one.” As time advances the record budget grows: distinctions become affordable, wave functions decohere and sharpen, and systems become separable and distinguishable — the “but separate.” At t = now, granularity is at its minimum and the wave function is at its sharpest. This is the same time-dependent granularity used in the Early & Distant Universe section.

What does “paying” for a distinction mean, exactly? It has a precise definition — see What it means to pay for something.

This item asks whether the Standard Model's gauge group — SU(3) × SU(2) × U(1), color, weak, and hypercharge multiplied together — has to show up in the candidate shape as three separate geometric pieces bolted together, or whether that “product” structure is just how a deeper, more unified geometry happens to look at everyday energies. A mathematical product of pieces comes with a built-in, canonical way of splitting back apart, so the question is whether that split is real or merely convenient.

Nonseparability is the relevant requirement here: no part of the geometry gets to be treated as independent from the rest for free — any such split has to be earned, not assumed. Worked through carefully, the three-factor product turns out to be allowed with no leftover unused pieces and no blending between the sectors, but only once the already-observed fact that the Standard Model's own gauge group factors that way is fed in as an input. So the product is paid for by observation, not conjured from the shape's rules alone, and the admissibility of splitting it into independent parts follows from the granularity cost-floor rather than being declared. The remaining question is about depth of geometric unity — a record-conditional research fork, non-blocking unless it changes a finite record — not about coarseness changing with distance from now.

Honest status: this is anchored, with one part open and shown, not rounded up. The paid part — “no spectator factors, no mixing” — is a solid, resolved result given the observed Standard Model content and the Nonseparability rule, with its admissibility following from the granularity cost-floor rather than assumed. The one open part is depth: whether this product is merely the low-energy face of a single, deeper, irreducible unified geometry is an explicitly open, unproven conjecture — no specific candidate for that deeper geometry has been produced or checked. So the defensible position is that the product structure is real and paid-for now, while the “interface to something deeper” reading is a named, record-conditional research fork — non-blocking unless it changes a finite record, and shown openly rather than folded into either “solved” or a bare “open.”

This item is about a specific kind of independence claim: could there be extra, disconnected pieces of matter — particles or sectors that couple to nothing else, sitting off to the side for no structural reason — hiding in the theory unnoticed? A “spectator sector” like that would be a free-rider: real in the model, but not required by, or connected to, anything actually observed. This check asks whether that kind of quiet addition is allowed.

The Nonseparability requirement says a piece of a system only gets to count as independent if that independence is earned, never assumed for free. An unpaid spectator sector is exactly the thing this rules out: an extra label, modulus, or excitation with no anchor in what is observed and no forced coupling to the rest of the structure. Because the elimination follows straight from that requirement — with no additional unproven assumption added on top — it belongs to the strongest, rarest category of result: forced by the constraints themselves rather than earned only by matching an observation. (This is separate from, and does not by itself explain, the count of matter generations, which rests on its own calculation and is reported elsewhere as resting on the observed particle content.)

Honest status: this elimination is resolved — forced by the constraints alone, not merely consistent with them and not an open question. It should not be over-read, though: it says unpaid, disconnected spectator sectors are excluded; it does not by itself fix how many generations of matter exist (that stays observation-earned) or settle other, related open questions about the deeper structure of the Standard Model's gauge sector, which remain separately and honestly labeled open elsewhere on this site.

The Separation Certificate is a fixed checklist of seven questions used to test any claim that one piece of physics is truly independent from another — two subsystems, two geometric factors, two decay channels, an observer and what it observes. Independence is never simply assumed here; a proposed split has to answer questions about which frozen object is being divided, whether it introduces a hidden new scale, whether a finite record actually proves the split exists, whether it survives changes of coordinates or convention, whether the split was fixed before the result it explains was already known, and whether its independence has actually been earned rather than presumed.

This checklist is the public, general-audience form of the program's foundational rule that independence from the rest of reality cannot be assumed, only earned. The “finite record” question is where time enters: the further a claim reaches from directly measured, present-day anchors, the coarser and less certain the finite record backing it becomes, so separations invoked to explain distant or early conditions are held to the same seven questions but typically have a harder time answering the granularity one cleanly. A “not yet” on any single question leaves that separation open rather than settled, no matter how convenient it would be to treat it as free.

Honest status: the certificate itself is a classification tool, not a discovery about nature, so it is resolved as a method — it is fully specified and has been applied to real cases with named, unrounded outcomes (see the Standard Model interface and Kinds of Separation pages). Whether any given separation it is applied to passes remains a separate, case-by-case question, and several of those individual cases are still open. The tool does not prove or disprove anything by itself; it only tells you honestly which of the seven questions still need an answer.
