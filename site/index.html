How was this even possible?

The honest answer is that the method is elimination, not invention. List what any theory of everything must deliver, eliminate every candidate that cannot survive the constraints, and keep the one shape that does — and elimination is exactly what machines are good at: enumerable, checkable, repeatable. The era made it feasible: an AI can hold the whole requirement bill at once, grind the elimination ledgers, and check every step, while the human supplies the questions, the standards, and the refusal to accept vibes. That last point turns the usual objection on its head — nothing here asks you to trust the author, because every step is machine-checkable precisely because it was machine-built. The discipline is what makes it credible: freeze-before-compare, fail-closed SHA-256 hashes, misses published on the front page, and a public list of the framework's own refuted claims — the process was built so that cheating would be visible. And the shape was found, not chosen: the cheaper-looking rival CP² was built end-to-end and broke at the gauge gate, while the survivor was frozen once and never re-tuned.

The arithmetic also runs the wrong way for a fabrication: a handful of measured anchors in, twenty-plus correct numbers out.
