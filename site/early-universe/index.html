Framing the beginning of the universe

This section asks what the candidate shape says about the earliest moments of the universe and its long history since — and holds every answer to the same discipline as the rest of this site: compute first, compare after, and report the honest status even when that status is "not yet," "blocked," or "open."

Read this before anything else here: nothing in this section is a confirmed prediction in the reserved sense. Some items are candidate structural outputs of the shape, checked blind against measured values. Others are explicitly blocked pending a computation that has not been finished. None are tuned to match data after the fact — see the blind prediction method for the discipline that enforces this.

Why the early universe is a hard test

Cosmology is one of the few places physics offers a genuinely blind test of a structural theory. The early universe left behind a small number of precisely measured numbers — the cosmic microwave background's spectrum and its tilt, the abundances of light elements, the pattern of large-scale structure, the expansion history recorded in supernovae and baryon acoustic oscillations. A candidate shape for the extra structure of the universe either reproduces the ingredients that predict these numbers, or it does not. There is no room to adjust the shape after seeing the answer without that adjustment being visible as after-the-fact tuning.

That is exactly why this section exists as its own area of the site, separate from the particle-physics tests in The Tests. Cosmological quantities are sensitive to the geometry's vacuum energy, its inflationary dynamics, and its expansion history all at once — they are a stress test of the whole construction, not just one gate.

What "the beginning" means here

Two separate questions are in play, and this site keeps them separate rather than blurring them together:

1. What set the expansion going — inflation

Did the candidate geometry supply a field capable of driving a period of accelerated expansion, and does the pattern that expansion would imprint on the cosmic microwave background match what has actually been measured? This is the subject of the inflation gate, tracked in the program's ledger as Gap-08.

2. What the universe does today — the cosmological constant and expansion history

Does the geometry supply a value for the vacuum energy (the cosmological constant, Λ) and an expansion history from that value, computed natively and only then compared to observed distances, the microwave background, and structure growth? This calculation exists as a fully specified pipeline — see the blind prediction method — but it has not run to completion, because its required input is not yet computed.

The honest state of this section, in one paragraph

Neither question above is closed. The cosmological-constant-to-expansion-history pipeline is built, specified, and held under a strict rule against loading observed data before a native prediction exists — but it is currently blocked, because the native value of Λ has not been computed. The inflation gate has one candidate that failed outright and is recorded as dead, and one candidate that survives as an exponential-plateau candidate with a projected, falsifiable range for the tensor-to-scalar ratio — a range a real, named upcoming satellite mission can rule out. Nothing here is a "prediction" in the sense a finished, published theory would use that word; every number is labeled with the status it actually has.
