Test 13 — GUT Symmetry-Breaking Relic Test

Any proposed grand-unified-theory (GUT) symmetry breaking must explain, or dilute away, the topological relics it inevitably produces — magnetic monopoles above all. This test asks whether the granularity doctrine's transition at the GUT scale is compatible with the near-total non-observation of such relics, using the standard Kibble-mechanism monopole-overproduction calculation and the standard inflationary dilution mechanism that resolves it.

Verdict: CLOSED-AS-COMPRESSED-RECORD Shared with ΛCDM
Read this before anything else on this page. A CLOSED-AS-COMPRESSED-RECORD verdict here means the granularity interpretation of this epoch is consistent with the standard resolution of the monopole problem — it is not a claim that this framework derives a specific GUT gauge group, a specific breaking chain, or a specific inflaton sector that performs the required dilution. This test is SHARED-WITH-ΛCDM: the monopole problem and its inflationary resolution are standard grand-unified-cosmology results, not something unique to this framework's geometry. This framework has not supplied its own GUT gauge group or breaking chain to check against homotopy-group topology; the calculation below uses the generic, textbook GUT-monopole numbers. A qualitative "inflation would dilute any such relic" story is not treated here as a calculation — the numbers below are worked explicitly.

1Verdict

CLOSED-AS-COMPRESSED-RECORD. This framework does not (at this stage) derive a specific GUT gauge group, a specific symmetry-breaking chain, or a from-first-principles inflaton sector — so it cannot yet be checked at the level of "which homotopy group, which topological defect, from which breaking." What it can be checked against is the generic, model-independent statement the granularity doctrine requires: that a proposed GUT-scale differentiation event does not silently overproduce relics that would already have been seen or would overclose the universe. That generic check passes: any GUT with a breaking chain producing 't~Hooft–Polyakov monopoles at \(M_{GUT}\sim10^{16}\) GeV overproduces them by roughly 20 orders of magnitude relative to the closure bound if unquenched, and a period of inflation with as few as ∼16 e-folds after monopole production is sufficient to dilute them below the closure bound — far fewer than the ∼50–60 e-folds standard inflation is independently required to supply for the horizon and flatness problems. So the record actually available is the compressed one: "monopoles are not observed, consistent with inflationary dilution," not a first-principles derivation of the breaking chain itself. That is precisely what CLOSED-AS-COMPRESSED-RECORD is for.

2Tested Claim

The precise claim under test: "A GUT-scale symmetry-breaking differentiation event is a valid granularity transition only if the topological relics (monopoles, domain walls, strings) it produces are either absent by the symmetry-breaking topology, or diluted/removed by a quantified mechanism consistent with their non-observation." If a GUT breaking chain produced monopoles with no dilution mechanism, the relic density would overclose the universe by many orders of magnitude — a direct contradiction with observation — and this test would report CONTRADICTED. If the breaking chain produces no monopoles (e.g., because the vacuum manifold has trivial second homotopy group) the test would report CLOSED outright. Because this framework has not committed to a specific GUT gauge group and breaking chain, the honest intermediate verdict is that the generic problem is solved by a known, quantified mechanism — hence "compressed record," not full derivation.

4Calculation Summary

Step 1 — window definition. \(W\) = {GUT-scale symmetry-breaking transition at \(T\sim T_{GUT}\sim2\times10^{16}\) GeV, radiation domination, prior to or coincident with the start of inflation}. The relevant process is the Kibble mechanism: as a gauge symmetry \(G\to H\) breaks at a phase transition, causally disconnected regions (of size at most the horizon) choose the vacuum orientation independently, and topological defects (monopoles, if \(\pi_2(G/H)\neq 0\)) are trapped at the boundaries between regions with roughly one defect per horizon volume.

Step 2 — homotopy / relic-type identification. For a generic simple GUT group (e.g. \(SU(5)\)) breaking to the Standard Model gauge group with its \(U(1)\) factor, the vacuum manifold has \(\pi_2(G/H)\cong\mathbb{Z}\), guaranteeing 't Hooft–Polyakov monopole production — this is the generic, model-independent obstruction the granularity doctrine must confront (this framework has not specified whether its own breaking chain avoids this via a non-simple or non-simply-connected group choice, so the generic case is used here rather than assumed away).

Step 3 — raw (undiluted) relic abundance. Horizon size at \(T_{GUT}\) under standard radiation-domination Hubble expansion, \(H\approx 1.66\sqrt{g_*}\,T^2/M_{Pl}\): \[ H(T_{GUT}) \approx 1.66\sqrt{106.75}\,\frac{(2\times10^{16}\,\text{GeV})^2}{1.22\times10^{19}\,\text{GeV}} \approx 5.6\times10^{14}\ \text{GeV}, \qquad d_H = H^{-1}\approx1.8\times10^{-15}\ \text{GeV}^{-1}. \] With roughly one monopole per horizon volume (\(n_{mono}\sim d_H^{-3}\)) and entropy density \(s=(2\pi^2/45)g_*T^3\), the monopole-to-entropy ratio at production is \[ \frac{n_{mono}}{s}\Big|_{\text{no dilution}} \approx 5\times10^{-7}. \] Converting to a present-day closure fraction using \(M_{mono}\approx5\times10^{17}\) GeV and \(s_0\approx2891\ \text{cm}^{-3}\) (assuming, as the naive comparison does, that \(n/s\) were conserved with no dilution) gives \[ \Omega_{mono}h^2\Big|_{\text{no dilution}} \sim 6\times10^{19}, \] compared to the observed total matter density \(\Omega_m h^2 = 0.143\) (Planck 2018). This is the textbook monopole-overproduction problem (Preskill 1979): roughly 20 orders of magnitude too many monopoles if no dilution mechanism intervenes.

Step 4 — does inflation dilute them? Comoving number density is diluted by \(e^{-3N}\) for \(N\) e-folds of inflation occurring after monopole production. Requiring \(\Omega_{mono}h^2\) to fall below an acceptable ceiling (taken generously here as the total matter density, \(0.143\), so any tighter observational bound only strengthens the conclusion): \[ e^{-3N} \lesssim \frac{0.143}{6\times10^{19}} \approx 2\times10^{-21} \quad\Rightarrow\quad N \gtrsim \frac{-\ln(2\times10^{-21})}{3} \approx 16. \] Standard inflationary cosmology independently requires \(N\sim50\text{–}60\) e-folds to solve the horizon and flatness problems (Test 04 of this suite; Liddle & Lyth 2000). Since \(50\text{–}60 \gg 16\), any inflationary episode long enough to solve the horizon problem is automatically long enough to dilute GUT monopoles produced before or during its first ∼16 e-folds to a cosmologically harmless density — provided the GUT transition occurs before or during early inflation, not after reheating.

Step 5 — record check. The fossil record here is a null result: no magnetic monopole has ever been detected. The MACRO experiment's final flux limit (\(\lesssim10^{-16}\ \text{cm}^{-2}\text{s}^{-1}\text{sr}^{-1}\), Eur. Phys. J. C 25, 511, 2002) and the independent Parker bound from the survival of galactic magnetic fields are both consistent with (and were historically part of the original motivation for) inflationary dilution of a GUT-era monopole population. This absence-of-relic is itself the observational record the granularity doctrine must be checked against.

Step 6 — cross-epoch consistency. Diluting monopoles by \(e^{-3N}\) with \(N\sim16\text{–}60\) does not disturb BBN (which depends on reheating after inflation restoring entropy, not on the pre-inflationary monopole abundance), CMB acoustic physics, or BAO/LSS — all of which depend on post-reheating physics that is set by reheating temperature and \(\eta=n_b/n_\gamma\) (Test 21 of this suite), not by the diluted relic density. No other test in this suite requires a pre-inflation monopole population to survive.

5Granularity Interpretation

Under the doctrine "cosmic history is the history of increasing recordable distinction," a GUT-scale symmetry-breaking event is a real granularity transition (the unified gauge symmetry differentiates into the Standard Model's product structure), but not every topological by-product of that transition survives to become a stable, recordable distinction. Monopoles produced by the Kibble mechanism are a genuine, quantifiable prediction of the breaking topology — but the rule "a distinction is valid in window \(W\) only if it can be coupled, encoded, stabilized, and/or observed at that window's physical resolution" is satisfied here in the negative: the calculation shows these relics are diluted below any observationally-accessible density by the time the window closes. The doctrine does not require that every topologically-possible defect become a permanent record; it requires that the theory correctly predict which of them do. Here, the generic prediction (monopoles are overproduced, then diluted below detectability) matches the generic observational record (none detected) — a compressed match, not a first-principles derivation of this framework's own breaking chain.

6Gate Routing

This test informs the GUT-to-sector differentiation gate (Test 13 of the Early Universe Granularity Test Suite), with primary terminal-anchor pressure on Distinction / Encoding (speculative ontology layer — interpretive label, not a physics result). Because the calculation above uses a generic GUT gauge group and the standard inflationary-dilution mechanism rather than a gauge group, breaking chain, or inflaton sector derived from this framework's own geometry, this closure sits on the SHARED-WITH-ΛCDM side of the ledger. It is a necessary-consistency check (the doctrine does not conflict with the standard monopole-problem resolution), not a framework-specific derivation of a GUT structure. It is closely coupled to Test 14 (Monopole Production and Dilution) and Test 15 (Cosmic Strings and Topological Defects), which carry the detailed defect-by-defect ledger, and to the framework-specific inflation-gate tests (07–09), where this framework's own inflaton-slope prediction is explicitly not geometrically forced (see the inflation gate, Gap-08).

7Failure Mode

This test did not fail outright, but it is intentionally graded as compressed rather than fully closed, for these reasons: (a) this framework has not supplied a specific GUT gauge group or breaking chain, so the homotopy-group calculation in Step 2 uses the generic textbook case rather than this framework's own vacuum manifold; (b) the inflaton sector performing the dilution in Step 4 is the standard, model-independent inflationary mechanism, not a framework-derived field; (c) no claimed monopole, string, or wall in this test is asserted as observed — the record used is a null result (non-detection) cross-checked with a numerical overproduction-then-dilution calculation, avoiding the specific failure mode of "a relic is asserted but no abundance calculation is supplied."

8Next Action

Derivation, not data lookup: the outstanding work is to specify this framework's own GUT (or GUT-like) gauge structure and breaking chain from its geometry, compute \(\pi_2(G/H)\) (and \(\pi_0\), \(\pi_1\) for domain walls and strings) for that specific chain, and re-run Steps 3–4 with the framework-specific monopole mass and production epoch rather than the generic \(2\times10^{16}\) GeV benchmark used here. Until that derivation exists, this test remains correctly labeled CLOSED-AS-COMPRESSED-RECORD: consistent with the standard resolution, not yet a framework-specific prediction. See also Tests 14 and 15 for the defect-type-specific follow-up ledger.

Bottom line

Grand-unified symmetry breaking generically overproduces magnetic monopoles by roughly twenty orders of magnitude relative to the observed matter density — and the standard inflationary mechanism, already required in far greater quantity (∼50–60 e-folds) to solve the horizon and flatness problems, comfortably dilutes them (only ∼16 e-folds needed) below any observational bound. The granularity doctrine survives this generic check. What remains open is framework-specific: this program has not yet derived its own GUT gauge group, breaking chain, or inflaton sector to check against this same arithmetic.
