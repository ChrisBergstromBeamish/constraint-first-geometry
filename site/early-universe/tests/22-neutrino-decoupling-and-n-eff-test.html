Test 22 — Neutrino Decoupling and N_eff Test

Do neutrinos become a separately-evolving relic sector — thermally decoupled from the photon bath but still gravitationally connected — at the correct epoch, leaving behind the correct effective radiation density \(N_{\rm eff}\)? Part of the 44-test Early Universe Granularity Test Suite, which checks whether the doctrine "cosmic history is the history of increasing recordable distinction" is consistent with standard cosmology and particle physics at each epoch — not whether it uniquely predicts that epoch.

Read this before the verdict: a CLOSED result on this page means the granularity interpretation — "neutrinos become a separate, thermally-disconnected relic sector at the weak-interaction freeze-out epoch, while remaining gravitationally connected" — is consistent with the standard thermal history of the universe and with the current measured value of \(N_{\rm eff}\). It is not a claim that this framework uniquely predicted neutrino decoupling or the number 3.044, and it is not a derivation of the weak coupling, the neutrino masses, or the number of neutrino species from this framework's own geometry. The decoupling temperature, the entropy-transfer calculation, and the Standard Model value of \(N_{\rm eff}\) are SHARED-WITH-ΛCDM — inherited directly from Standard Model weak interactions and standard thermal-history cosmology, not something specific to this framework's geometry. A qualitative match only motivates the route; it never proves it.

Verdict: CLOSED-AS-COMPRESSED-RECORD   SHARED-WITH-ΛCDM

1. Verdict

CLOSED-AS-COMPRESSED-RECORD. The framework does not derive the weak interaction coupling, the neutrino decoupling temperature, or the Standard Model particle content (three light neutrino species) from its own geometry — those are taken wholesale from the electroweak sector of the Standard Model and standard thermal-history cosmology. What the test can honestly check, and what does check out numerically, is the timing and consistency claim: that neutrinos stop exchanging energy with the photon-electron- positron plasma (the weak interaction rate falls below the Hubble rate) at the correct temperature, that the subsequent \(e^+e^-\) annihilation dumps entropy into photons but not into the already-decoupled neutrinos (producing the well-known photon/neutrino temperature ratio), and that the resulting predicted effective number of relativistic species, \(N_{\rm eff}=3.044\) (Standard Model, no extra light species), is consistent with the current best measurements from both the CMB and Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN). No extra light relativistic species and no anomalous decoupling behavior is required or indicated.

2. Tested Claim

The precise claim under test: "The three Standard Model neutrino species, in thermal equilibrium with the photon-electron-positron plasma via weak interactions at high temperature, decouple (freeze out) from that plasma at \(T_\nu^{\rm dec}\sim2\)–\(3\ {\rm MeV}\), when the weak interaction rate drops below the Hubble expansion rate. After decoupling, neutrinos free-stream and evolve as a separate relic sector, connected to the rest of the universe only gravitationally, while \(e^+e^-\) annihilation shortly afterward (\(T\sim m_e\approx0.511\ {\rm MeV}\)) transfers entropy into the photon bath but not (fully) into the neutrino bath, producing a predicted photon-to-neutrino temperature ratio and an effective relativistic species count \(N_{\rm eff}=3.044\), consistent with observed CMB and BBN constraints."

This is evaluated as a consistency and timing claim, not a derivation claim: the framework does not compute the weak coupling \(G_F\), the number of neutrino species, or the neutrino masses from its own geometry. It claims only that its granularity story — "neutrinos become a separate, distinguishable, thermally-disconnected sector at freeze-out, still coupled to the rest of the universe only through gravity" — does not conflict with what Standard Model weak-interaction physics and precision cosmology jointly require.

4. Calculation Summary

Step 1 — window definition. Define the physical window \(W = \{t\approx0.4\text{--}6\ {\rm s},\ T\approx0.5\text{--}3\ {\rm MeV},\ \text{weak-interaction freeze-out and }e^+e^-\text{ annihilation},\ \text{radiation-dominated expansion}\}\). This spans neutrino decoupling (\(T\sim2\)–\(3\ {\rm MeV}\), \(t\sim1\ {\rm s}\)) through \(e^+e^-\) annihilation (\(T\sim m_e/3\sim0.2\)–\(0.5\ {\rm MeV}\), \(t\sim6\ {\rm s}\)), immediately before BBN begins (\(t\sim1\)–\(3\ {\rm min}\)).

Step 2 — granularity condition. Above \(T_\nu^{\rm dec}\), neutrinos are tightly coupled to the photon-electron-positron plasma via weak interactions (\(\nu e\leftrightarrow\nu e\), \(e^+e^-\leftrightarrow\nu\bar\nu\)) and share a common temperature with it — they are not a separately trackable thermal sector. Below \(T_\nu^{\rm dec}\), the weak interaction rate has fallen below the Hubble rate, and neutrinos free-stream: they become a distinguishable, separately-evolving relic sector with their own temperature history, still gravitationally coupled to everything else (via their contribution to the total energy density and hence \(H(t)\)) but no longer thermally exchanging energy with photons or baryons. This is the "Separate but Connected" transition the test is built to check.

Step 3 — rate/threshold check. The weak interaction rate for processes like \(\nu e\leftrightarrow\nu e\) scales as \(\Gamma_\nu \sim G_F^2 T^5\), while the Hubble rate in the radiation era scales as \(H\sim 1.66\sqrt{g_*}\,T^2/M_{\rm Pl}\). Setting \(\Gamma_\nu = H\) and solving for \(T\) with \(G_F = 1.166\times10^{-5}\ {\rm GeV}^{-2}\) (PDG 2024) gives the standard textbook result \(T_\nu^{\rm dec}\approx1\ {\rm MeV}\times(G_F^2 M_{\rm Pl})^{-1/3}\)-type scaling, numerically evaluated in the literature (Kolb & Turner 1990; Enqvist, Kainulainen & Maalampi 1992; Dolgov 2002) to \(T_\nu^{\rm dec}\approx2\)–\(3\ {\rm MeV}\), with \(\nu_e\) decoupling marginally later than \(\nu_\mu,\nu_\tau\) because of its additional charged-current coupling to electrons. This is well above the electron mass (\(m_e=0.511\ {\rm MeV}\)), so neutrino decoupling completes before \(e^+e^-\) annihilation begins in earnest — the ordering required for the standard entropy-transfer argument below to hold.

Step 4 — entropy transfer and \(N_{\rm eff}\). Because neutrinos have already decoupled when \(e^+e^-\) pairs annihilate near \(T\sim m_e\), the entropy released by annihilation (\(s = \tfrac{2\pi^2}{45}g_{*s}T^3\)) heats the photon bath but not the neutrino bath. Entropy conservation in the photon-electron-positron sector before and after annihilation (\(g_{*s}^{\rm before}=2+\tfrac74\times4=\tfrac{11}{2}\) → \(g_{*s}^{\rm after}=2\)) gives the standard instantaneous-decoupling ratio \((T_\nu/T_\gamma)^3 = 4/11\), i.e. \(T_\nu/T_\gamma\approx0.7138\). A more careful, non-instantaneous kinetic-theory treatment — which accounts for the small residual coupling during annihilation and finite-temperature QED corrections to the photon plasma — raises the Standard Model prediction slightly above the naive 3 neutrino species to \(N_{\rm eff}=3.044\) (Bennett et al. 2021; consistent 3.043, Akita & Yamaguchi 2020), replacing older calculations that gave \(N_{\rm eff}\approx 3.046\) (Mangano et al. 2005). This 3.044 is a Standard-Model calculation, not a free parameter and not something this framework computes independently.

Step 5 — record check / cross-epoch consistency. \(N_{\rm eff}\) leaves two independent fossil records: (a) it changes the Hubble expansion rate during BBN, altering the neutron-to-proton freeze-out ratio and hence the helium-4 and deuterium abundances — the BBN-inferred value is \(N_{\rm eff}=2.86^{+0.27}_{-0.28}\) (Fields et al. 2020); and (b) it changes the CMB damping tail and the early-ISW effect — the Planck 2018 CMB+BAO value is \(N_{\rm eff}=2.99\pm0.17\). Both independent measurements, separated by roughly 380,000 years of cosmic history and probing completely different physics (nuclear reaction rates vs. photon diffusion damping), are statistically consistent with each other and with the Standard Model prediction of 3.044, to within \(\lesssim1\sigma\). No extra light relativistic species (e.g. a sterile neutrino, a light dark-sector particle, or a modified decoupling history) is required or favored by current data. This closes without tension against BBN, CMB, or the standard thermal-history picture.

5. Granularity Interpretation

What newly becomes distinguishable/recordable at this transition: before \(T_\nu^{\rm dec}\), neutrinos are not a separately-trackable sector — they share a single common temperature and equilibrium distribution with photons, electrons, and positrons, exchanging energy freely. After decoupling, neutrinos become a distinguishable relic population with their own free-streaming distribution function and their own temperature history, forever after connected to the rest of the universe only through gravity (their contribution to the total energy density, and via free-streaming damping imprinted on the CMB and structure growth) — never again through direct thermal contact until, if ever, a future detector directly measures the cosmic neutrino background (see the companion Test 41). This is the clean "Separate but Connected" case the test suite calls for: a sector that is thermally cut off but remains gravitationally coupled and hence indirectly recordable through \(N_{\rm eff}\)'s imprint on BBN and the CMB.

7. Failure Mode

This test does not rise to a framework-specific derivation, for one honest reason:

  • No derived weak coupling or species count. \(G_F\), the neutrino decoupling temperature, and the count of three light neutrino species are Standard Model inputs, not outputs of this framework's geometry. The framework supplies only the interpretive claim — "neutrinos become a separate, distinguishable, thermally-disconnected but gravitationally-connected relic sector at freeze-out" — and that claim is checked for consistency against BBN and CMB data, not derived from first principles here.

No numerical exclusion or conflict was found: the freeze-out rate comparison, the entropy-transfer calculation giving \(N_{\rm eff}=3.044\), and the independent BBN and CMB measurements of \(N_{\rm eff}\) all check out against dated, sourced values, with both measurements statistically consistent with the Standard Model prediction and with each other. Because the check passes and no data conflict exists, the outcome is not CONTRADICTED; because the framework does not derive the weak coupling or the species count itself, this is graded CLOSED-AS-COMPRESSED-RECORD rather than a framework-specific CLOSED.

8. Next Action

  • Data lookup: monitor tightening \(N_{\rm eff}\) constraints from CMB-S4 (projected \(\sigma(N_{\rm eff})\sim0.03\)–\(0.06\)) and from improved BBN abundance determinations, which will sharpen the test's discriminating power for any small deviation from 3.044.

  • Terminal-anchor routing: the "decoupling makes the neutrino sector lawfully encodable as a separate relic record" framing remains at the Distinction / Encoding anchor as a speculative interpretive layer; it is not promoted to a physics closure by this test.

Bottom line

Standard weak-interaction physics places neutrino decoupling at \(T_\nu^{\rm dec}\approx2\)– \(3\ {\rm MeV}\) (\(t\sim1\ {\rm s}\)), ahead of \(e^+e^-\) annihilation at \(T\sim m_e=0.511\ {\rm MeV}\), giving the Standard Model prediction \(N_{\rm eff}=3.044\) (Bennett et al. 2021). Both independent cosmological probes of this number — BBN, \(N_{\rm eff}=2.86^{+0.27}_{-0.28}\) (Fields et al. 2020), and the CMB, \(N_{\rm eff}=2.99\pm0.17\) (Planck 2018) — are statistically consistent with 3.044 and with each other, with no evidence for extra light relativistic species. This framework's granularity reading of decoupling — "neutrinos become a separate, thermally-disconnected but gravitationally-connected relic sector at freeze-out" — is consistent with that established picture. It does not derive the weak coupling, the decoupling temperature, or the number of neutrino species, and the relevant physics here is inherited wholesale from the Standard Model and standard thermal-history cosmology (SHARED-WITH-ΛCDM), not specific to this framework's geometry.
