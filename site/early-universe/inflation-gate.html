The inflation gate — Gap-08

Did the candidate geometry supply a field capable of driving inflation — the brief burst of accelerated expansion widely thought to have set the initial conditions for the observable universe — and does the pattern that field would leave in the cosmic microwave background match what has actually been measured? This page reports the gate's status exactly as it stands today: one candidate dead, one candidate surviving, and the surviving candidate held at candidate grade, not prediction grade.

Headline status: Gap-08 is CLOSED-NEGATIVE on the specific question of whether the frozen geometry forces a unique inflaton slope. It does not. The internal-curvature term that distinguishes the geometry's breathing directions was checked directly, and it gives canonical slopes λ²_K6 = 8/3, λ²_S2 = 4, and λ²_uniform = 22/9 for the three ways of counting the internal directions — none of which equals the 1/6 this page previously reported as the geometry's forced output. That number was a declared convention (λ² := 4/K_σσ), not the slope the geometry's own curvature actually produces. The framework does not currently supply a forced inflaton slope, and no replacement number is offered in its place. What survives this finding untouched is the branch-independent falsifier: a tensor-to-scalar ratio range r ∈ [3.5, 36]×10⁻³ that does not depend on which slope convention is chosen, and that a real, dated satellite mission (LiteBIRD, ~2030) can still test directly.

1. Two candidate inflatons, one dead, one alive

The candidate geometry offers two internal degrees of freedom that could in principle have driven inflation — informally called the "breathing modes" of the compact internal geometry. Each was checked as a candidate inflaton, independently:

The ρ-inflaton — DEAD

This candidate is too stiff to inflate: its internal restoring force (its Kaluza–Klein stiffness) is too strong for the slow, gradual rolling that inflation requires. This verdict is recorded at qualitative grade — the mode is disqualified in kind, not by a razor-thin numeric miss — and is treated as sufficient to retire this candidate. No sharper, certificate-grade kill has been computed, and none is needed: the gate does not depend on it.

The σ-inflaton — SURVIVES, slope NOT forced

This candidate produces an exponential-plateau potential — the same broad family of inflation models (α-attractor-like) that independently fits the observed cosmic microwave background well. A direct check of the frozen geometry's internal-curvature term shows it does not force a single slope: the three ways of counting the compact space's breathing directions give three different canonical slopes (8/3, 4, 22/9), and the number this page previously reported (1/6) was a declared convention, not one of them. The candidate itself is not dead — the exponential-plateau shape still stands, and the branch-independent r falsifier below is unaffected — but the framework does not currently supply a forced value for the slope.

2. What was checked, and what it found

This page previously reported the plateau's slope as fixed by the geometry via λ² = 4/K_σσ = 1/6, with a spectral index landing at the dead center of the Planck-measured range. That claim has since been checked directly against the frozen 13-dimensional geometry's own internal-curvature term — the term that actually distinguishes the compact space's "breathing" directions from one another — and the result is negative: the geometry's own curvature does not produce a slope of 1/6 under any of the three natural ways of counting those directions.

K_σσ = 24 is a genuine radion coefficient that comes out of the geometry — that part of the earlier computation was not wrong. What was not correct was treating λ² := 4/K_σσ as though it were the geometry's own curvature slope. It is a convention that happens to coincide with the geometry's slope only in the case of no positive-curvature breathing direction at all — not the case that applies here. The honest conclusion is that the framework does not currently supply a forced inflaton slope. No replacement number is offered in its place; supplying one would require further input (a loop coefficient and additional modulus data) that has not been computed. The spectral-index projection that depended on 1/6 is retracted along with it.

3. Why the falsifier is a range, and why it survives the slope finding

Section 2 above retired the claim that the geometry forces a single slope. That retirement changes what this program can say about a single projected value for r — it does not change the branch-independent falsifier. The tensor-to-scalar ratio was always reported as a range precisely because it also depends on a normalization convention inside the geometry's kinetic-term bookkeeping that has not been pinned to a single choice, independent of the slope question. Combining the several ways that convention could resolve with the several canonical slopes the geometry's curvature actually produces (Section 2) still lands inside the same union range this program pre-registered before this finding:

Branch-independent falsifier, unchanged: r ∈ [3.5, 36]×10⁻³. This is the number that matters for the honest falsifiability claim, because it does not depend on resolving the normalization-convention question below, and it was constructed wide enough to already absorb the range of canonical slopes the closed-negative finding in Section 2 turned up.

What this page can no longer report is a single narrower "operative branch" projection or a spectral-index value asserted to sit at the dead center of the Planck-measured range — both of those depended on treating λ² = 1/6 as the geometry's forced output, which Section 2 retracts. The wider, branch-independent range is the honest claim that remains standing.

4. The falsifier: LiteBIRD, ~2030

A real, named, dated test

LiteBIRD is a planned satellite mission expected to measure the polarization of the cosmic microwave background with enough sensitivity to constrain the tensor-to-scalar ratio far below today's bound, around 2030.

  • If LiteBIRD measures r outside [3.5, 36]×10⁻³, the σ-inflaton candidate is falsified — regardless of which slope or normalization convention turns out to be correct. This is a real, pre-registered, binary risk the candidate is exposed to.
  • If LiteBIRD measures r inside that union range, the σ-inflaton's exponential-plateau shape survives, though — per Section 2 — this program does not currently have a forced slope prediction that would let a measurement inside the range be read as confirming a specific value.

Until that measurement happens, this gate cannot close by computation alone. Put plainly: this gap does not close on computation alone — r is an observable that only a real experiment can pin down.

5. Why this page said "candidate," not "prediction" — and why that caution was earned

This page has always avoided calling the σ-inflaton's spectral numbers a "prediction" in the sense that word carries once a result is locked down and certificate-grade. That caution turned out to be the right call: the specific claim under question — that the plateau's slope is forced by the geometry, with no adjustable dial — was checked directly against the frozen geometry's own curvature term and did not survive the check (Section 2). Had this page called λ² = 1/6 a "prediction," that would now be an outright wrong prediction on the record; because it was held at CANDIDATE / PROJECTED grade pending confirmation, the correct outcome is a clean retraction of that specific number, not a false claim requiring a correction to a "result." The exponential-plateau shape and the branch-independent r falsifier are unaffected and remain at CANDIDATE / PROJECTED grade, exactly as strong as they have actually earned.

6. What else is open, named plainly

The slope-forcing question itself is no longer open — Section 2 above closes it, negatively. Replacing the retired 1/6 slope with a forced value the geometry actually supports is OPEN-BLOCKED: it needs a loop-level coefficient (c_loop) and additional three-modulus data that have not been computed. Beyond that, the gate carries a small number of other named open items, each bounded and specific rather than a vague "more work needed":

  • The absolute amplitude of the scalar spectrum (A_s) is not yet computed — it depends on one further loop-level coefficient and one confirmatory read of a frozen internal-geometry file, neither of which is done yet.
  • The number of e-folds of inflation (N_*) that the reported bands assume has not been published alongside the bands themselves — the bands are reported as computed, but their dependence on the exact e-fold count is an open consistency question, not a hidden free parameter tuned after the fact.
  • A separate, longer-horizon question about the global stability of the compact internal geometry at very short distances is on record as open and tied to the same difficulty behind the cosmological-constant problem — but it is explicitly non-blocking for the testable spectrum reported above, which lives in a region of the theory this deeper question does not reach.

None of these open items change the falsifier: the union range [3.5, 36]×10⁻³ for LiteBIRD holds regardless of how they resolve.

7. Bottom line

One candidate inflaton is dead on qualitative grounds and is not being revived. The other, the σ-inflaton, keeps its exponential-plateau shape, but the claim that the geometry forces a unique slope for that plateau is now CLOSED-NEGATIVE: the frozen geometry's own curvature term gives canonical slopes of 8/3, 4, and 22/9 depending on how the internal breathing directions are counted, and the previously reported 1/6 was a convention that matches none of them. No forced slope, and no spectral-index projection built on one, is currently supplied. What survives untouched is the branch-independent, pre-registered falsifiable range for the tensor-to-scalar ratio, r ∈ [3.5, 36]×10⁻³, which a real, dated satellite mission (LiteBIRD, ~2030) will test directly. Every number on this page is reported at the strength it has actually earned — CANDIDATE and PROJECTED, or retracted outright where a check failed, never PREDICTED or CONFIRMED — and the gate's overall status remains OPEN pending that measurement, with this specific slope-forcing question now closed negative rather than pending.
