2. The community gap

2.1 The precise open problem

Theories of fundamental physics routinely overclaim. Two failure modes recur, and they are mirror images:

  1. Borrowing an excluded sector to rescue a result. A framework quietly leans on a problem it has declared out of scope to make a required result come out right — the excluded sector silently does load-bearing work on a claim the framework is making.
  2. Re-filing a hard required problem as "out of scope." A framework that should deliver some result instead moves it into the "not claimed" pile, narrowing its own scope after the fact to dodge a failure.

The field has no mechanical, checkable statement of exactly what a framework does and does not claim. Scope honesty is left to authorial restraint, and catching overreach is a manual job no reviewer can do exhaustively across a long manuscript with many appendices and certificates. The reviewer's burden is asymmetric: the author knows where every dependency lives; the reviewer must reconstruct the dependency graph by hand and hope to spot the one citation that crosses a scope line. In practice almost no one publishes a both-direction, falsifiable scope contract. Frameworks state what they claim; very few state, as a bound rule, what they refuse to claim and how that refusal is enforced.

2.2 Why this is genuinely a gap and not a triviality

It is tempting to dismiss scope disclosure as bookkeeping. It is not, for a structural reason: a long unification program's honesty is a graph property, not a per-statement property. A single certificate can be locally honest (every line true) while the program cheats globally (a Diagnostic-only object, introduced honestly in one appendix, is later cited as support for a required closure in another). No amount of careful per-statement writing catches this; only a check over the whole dependency graph does. The gap is therefore real: the community lacks a mechanizable notion of scope honesty for fundamental-physics manuscripts, and lacks the convention of publishing one.

This matters most precisely for ambitious frameworks. The more a theory claims to unify, the larger its surface area for quiet scope creep, and the more a reader needs a machine-checkable boundary to trust the headline. A "theory of everything" with no published boundary is unfalsifiable on scope by construction — you cannot catch it borrowing from a sector it never admitted exists.

2.3 State of the art / best practice today

The honest state of the art is prose disclaimers. Good papers include a "limitations" or "what we do not claim" section. Two things are almost always missing:

  • Both directions. Limitations sections name what is unsolved; they rarely bind the symmetric clause (a required result may not be quietly re-filed as a limitation after it fails).
  • A falsifier with teeth. A prose limitation is not falsifiable; it is an assertion. There is no stated event that, if observed, downgrades a specific claim.

SG-10's contribution against this baseline is to (a) publish a structured boundary ledger rather than prose, (b) bind both directions as load-bearing clauses, and (c) attach a falsifier whose penalty falls inward on the offending required gate. The companion reader module (Appendix CR11) frames the two classic failure modes of ambitious papers and positions the boundary against them. The cross-framework comparison of disclosure practice lives in BATTLE_GATES/ (not in this gate's certificate), where the observation is recorded that most rival geometries silently omit an exclusion list — which is why this gate registers as a disclosure-practice win there. That observation is a meta-comparison of disclosure honesty, never a physics determination (see §7.4).

2.4 Prior attempts and why each falls short

  • Authorial-restraint limitations sections. Fall short on mechanizability: a reviewer cannot run them, and they bind only one direction.
  • Per-result caveats scattered through a paper. Fall short on completeness: a reader cannot tell whether the caveats are exhaustive, because there is no single ledger to attack.
  • "Future work" framing. Fall short on honesty teeth: framing a required gap as future work is exactly the re-filing move clause (ii) is built to forbid.

SG-10 is the attempt to fix all three at once — a single ledger (completeness can be attacked in one place), two bound clauses (both directions), and an inward-falling falsifier (real teeth). Its honest residual is that the enforcement program is specified but not yet independently re-run; that residual is the load-bearing content of §6.

4. The insights we used

These are the moves that make the gate believable and reproducible. They are shareable in full — the physics and the method, not any device.

4.1 A disclosure gate's failure mode is the inverse of target-fitting

The program's central anti-cheat discipline elsewhere is the κ³·π⁻¹ falsification test: never write a quantity that is only writable because you already know the target (a number reverse-engineered to hit a measured value is "true by construction," not derived). SG-10 has no input to shrink and no number to derive, so the falsification test applies here in a negative form. The failure mode of a disclosure gate is not over-fitting a number; it is quiet scope creep — a wall labeled more-closed-than-it-is, a Diagnostic mode doing load-bearing work, a sibling paper claiming what Paper I excludes.

The operational consequence is the deflation-honesty check: every closure path on this gate must be checked for whether it states each wall at its campaign-conservative disposition, or whether it lets an attractive partial result read as more than it is. Stating a wall as more closed than its owner's campaign verified it is the SG-10-specific κ³·π⁻¹ violation. This single reframing is what turns "write a limitations section" into a checkable discipline.

4.2 Point the penalty inward

The deepest design insight is the downgrade asymmetry (§3.4). A disclosure layer naïvely has no teeth — it makes no claim, so there is nothing to falsify. The move that gives it teeth without making it a physics claim is to redirect the penalty: a scope violation downgrades the offending required gate, not the disclosure gate. This converts a passive limitations section into an active guardrail with leverage on ten other gates. It is the structural reason SG-10 can be both "closes no physics" and "load-bearing."

4.3 Both directions, or it does not bind

A one-direction limitations section ("here is what we don't solve") is honest but un-binding: it cannot stop the re-filing move. Binding both clauses (excluded↛required AND required↛excluded) closes the loophole where a framework, on encountering a failure in a required gate, quietly relocates the gate into the "not claimed" pile. The symmetry is what makes the boundary hard to game from either side.

4.4 The completeness witness, not the completeness claim

"Every relevant non-GUT sector is named" is a universal over an open-ended sector space — unprovable as a theorem (see §7.1). The believable move is to not claim the theorem and instead build a completeness witness: enumerate the standard non-GUT sectors and assign each to (a) a ledger row, (b) a defensible "subsumed-by" justification, or (c) a flagged true gap — and then issue an open challenge ("name one un-listed relevant non-GUT sector"). This converts an unprovable universal into a standing, attackable artifact at a declared scope-universe. The honest ceiling is AXIOM-CLOSED relative to the declared seven-sector universe, not exhaustive over all conceivable physics.

4.5 The firewall test for sector classification

For any candidate sector, the discriminating question is sharp: is it a required scoped-GUT gate (then it belongs in Gates 1–10, not in exclusions), or a genuinely non-GUT sector (then it belongs in §9.4)? This single test is what correctly keeps the unknown seesaw scale M_R as an SG-8 required-gate residual rather than relocating it into the SG-10 exclusion list — relocation that clause (ii) forbids exactly. The firewall test is the operational core of the completeness sweep (§6.1).

4.6 The real-wall / fake-wall discriminator (audit the implicit assumption)

The cleanest method-insight surfaced through the Λ wall. The cosmological-constant catastrophe — the naïve ~10^120 mismatch — rested on the absolute vacuum energy. But every zero-point-energy effect ever measured probes only differences (Casimir, Lamb shift, running): the absolute vacuum energy is unmeasured. Drop the unmeasured premise and the catastrophe-half dissolves — it was a fake wall, an artifact of an implicit unmeasured assumption. What remains is the observed small Λ, a separate, Weinberg-irreducible residue. This is the cleanest application of the audit-the-implicit-assumption method in the corpus, and it is exactly why SG-10's per-wall map must distinguish dissolved-not-solved from open: Λ's catastrophe-half is DISSOLVED; Λ's value is measured-but-irreducible / Weinberg-OPEN. (This insight is owned by the Λ campaign / Paper IV; SG-10 only fences and labels it.)
