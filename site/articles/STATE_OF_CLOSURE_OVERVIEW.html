State of the Closure

An honest map of where every gate in the program now sits — last reviewed 2026-06-27.

This is a constraint-first geometric framework that attempts to derive the Standard Model's structure — its gauge group, three generations, charge quantization, and forbidden decays — from one frozen higher-dimensional geometry, given a small set of measured inputs, and to interface that geometry honestly with quantum field theory and gravity. The page below states, in plain language and without inflation, exactly what is established, what is a construction we have specified but not finished, and what remains a genuine frontier research problem. The honest overall ceiling is unchanged: a serious candidate, not a validated theory — with no live refutation standing against it.

The one-paragraph state of play

The program reduces to one (still-open) buildable object, one construction, and three named frontier problems — with everything else honestly derived-given-those, and no live refutation standing against it.

That is the whole claim, stated at its true strength and no higher:

  • BUILDABLE (NOT BUILT) — one computational object is the most tractable open item: the short-distance (heat-kernel) expansion coefficient on the frozen geometry that the coupling-running, the graviton self-energy, and several gravity gates all lean on. It is a well-posed calculation, not a conceptual gap — but it is not built: the most recent attempt evaluated it two independent ways and the two routes disagreed, localizing a fault in one of them. So this object is open / near-built, and it currently discharges no gate.
  • CONSTRUCTION — one object is specified but not yet assembled end-to-end: the boundary anomaly-descent object that would turn the forbidden-operator, anomaly-cancellation, and scope-boundary arguments into a single machine-checked certificate. The component pieces exist; the master assembly is the open work.
  • FRONTIER — three problems are not ours to hand-wave: they are among the field's own hardest open questions. We publish them as open, with no claim to have solved them.
  • DERIVED-GIVEN-E — everything else (the gauge algebra, three generations, the hypercharge lattice, charge embedding, the flavor ratio/mixing structure, proton-stable operator structure) is shown to follow rigidly given the observed spectrum and the frozen geometry. "Given-E" is stated honestly: these results consume measured input; they do not conjure the spectrum from nothing.

"No live refutation" means exactly that — no current result inside the program contradicts observation or contradicts another gate. It is not a claim of proof. Two gates even carry standing, falsifiable precision tensions that we publish openly rather than hide (the electroweak custodial parameter, and a forced up-quark mass); see the table.

The three buckets, in detail

BUILDABLE (NOT BUILT)  One computational object — open / near-built (NOT built)

A single short-distance expansion coefficient (a heat-kernel / Seeley–DeWitt coefficient of the graviton operator on the frozen geometry, with its boundary-defect contribution) is the most tractable open item in the whole program, and it is shared across many gates: coupling running, the graviton self-energy, the ultraviolet question, black-hole entropy, and more — "computed once, used in many places." Where this stands honestly: the scale-free content is genuinely derived and cross-checked (an exact color factor, exact-rational test-geometry checks reproduced two independent ways, and the signs of the corrections), and a real curvature-input error was found and corrected this cycle. But the decisive numeric value is not in hand: when the shared boundary-defect coefficient was evaluated two structurally independent ways, the two routes disagreed, and the cross-check localized a fault in one route. Because the two routes did not agree, the object is audit-unverified and not built — it discharges no gate. This sector is published as open / diagnostic-only. It is a build, not a mystery, but it is honestly still open.

CONSTRUCTION  One object to assemble

The forbidden-decay (proton-stability) results, the higher-dimensional anomaly-cancellation gate, and the scope-boundary ledger all lean on the same underlying object: a boundary anomaly-descent construction that would certify, in one place and by machine, which operators are excluded to all orders, that every reduction anomaly cancels, and which sectors the theory does and does not claim. The component derivations are in hand — the low-dimension forbidden operators are machine-certified given the spectrum, one decay channel is excluded to all orders by a colour-triality argument, and the standard perturbative anomalies cancel given the spectrum. What remains is the master assembly: the all-order completeness write-up, the existence proof of the correct boundary-and-coset anomaly invariant (an integrality condition on the actual flux data plus the orbifold-wall boundary structure), and a single machine-checked consistency pass — none yet executed. Published as open pending that assembly.

FRONTIER  Three named research problems

These are not bookkeeping gaps. Each is, in its own right, a hard open problem in mathematical physics, and we make no claim to have closed any of them:

  • The ultraviolet fixed point (asymptotic safety). Whether the framework has a well-defined short-distance completion — a truncation-independent non-Gaussian fixed point — is the same class of question as the asymptotic-safety program for gravity. A candidate exists only at a declared truncation; current evidence trends against the naive route. Published open.
  • Dynamical fermion mirror-decoupling. The topological count gives three left-handed generations and no mirror partners at the level of indices; showing the mirror sector actually decouples dynamically through quantization is an open, strong-coupling problem that no topological certificate can settle (a light vector-like mirror pair is invisible to anomaly, cobordism and index tools). Published open.
  • The Yang–Mills mass gap. The confinement / mass-gap input the program relies on downstream is one of the field's canonical unsolved problems (a Clay Millennium problem). We reduce the entire continuum obligation to a single named, value-free uniform inequality and say so; the underlying problem is published open.

Publishing these as frontier problems is the honest move: a candidate theory that quietly "closed" the Yang–Mills mass gap or the UV completion in passing would be telling you something false about itself.

Everything else is honestly derived-given-those. That is the entire distance between "candidate" and "validated," and there is no live refutation standing against any of it.

What we are not claiming

  • We are not claiming the shared heat-kernel object is built — its last two-route evaluation disagreed, so it discharges no gate and every residual that leans on it stays open.
  • We are not claiming the geometry is proven unique or forced — only that it is the shortest survivor inside a stated, extensible grammar (SG-1, Deep Roots).
  • We are not claiming to derive the observed spectrum from nothing — the spectrum and a handful of measured constants are anchors that go in.
  • We are not claiming to have solved the Yang–Mills mass gap, the UV completion, or dynamical mirror-decoupling. Those are published as frontier problems.
  • Two gates carry standing, falsifiable precision tensions we publish rather than tune away: the electroweak custodial parameter (ρ ≠ 1 at tree level on the frozen object, SG-5) and the forced up-quark mass (a ~4.4σ tension in SG-8 that no adjustment can rescue).

If any one of those caveats were quietly dropped, the program would be over-claiming. Keeping them visible is what makes the rest of the page trustworthy.
