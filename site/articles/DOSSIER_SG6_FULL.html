2. The community gap

2.1 The precise open problem

A predictive compactification cannot have its hidden shape and size drifting freely: every modulus any later gate reads must be pinned before a number is compared. "Pinned" has a strong and a weak meaning, and the gap is the distance between them:

  1. Location (the weak, achievable part). The moduli sit at a definite point of moduli space — a critical point of the effective potential, ∂V = 0.
  2. Stability (the hard part). That point is a true minimum: the Hessian ∂²V is positive-definite in every modulus direction (no flat directions, no tachyonic/runaway directions), and this holds in a controlled way — ideally globally over the whole moduli space, not just in a neighborhood.

The community problem is (2) done globally and controllably. Location is comparatively cheap (symmetry often hands it to you); proving the full positive-definite Hessian, with all quantum corrections under control and no runaway at the boundaries of moduli space, is what nobody has delivered.

2.2 State of the art / best bound

This is a genuine textbook SHARED-OPEN wall. The honest cross-framework verdict is TIE / SHARED-OPEN — not a framework win:

  • String/flux compactifications stabilize moduli with fluxes and nonperturbative effects (the KKLT and Large-Volume programs), but full control over all moduli, including the de Sitter uplift and the absence of runaways, remains contested — the "swampland" discourse is precisely the admission that controlled, global stabilization is hard and possibly obstructed.
  • M-theory / G₂ compactifications face the same moduli-potential control problem.
  • F-theory inherits the flux-stabilization difficulties.
  • Noncommutative geometry fixes much of the structure spectrally but does not deliver a controlled global moduli potential with a proven positive-definite Hessian.
  • Lattice constructions discretize but do not solve the continuum stabilization in closed form.

So the best bound available to anyone is "local/chamber-restricted control under declared assumptions" — which is exactly the honest ceiling SG-6 claims.

4. The insights we used

These are the moves that made the progress believable and reproducible — shared so a reader can check and extend them.

  1. Witness-TYPE over witness-value. The decisive design choice is to test the kind of pin, not its number. A type-check makes "minimize-at-answer" inadmissible by construction, which is a far stronger anti-tuning guarantee than any sensitivity bound. This is the y = R² curve-not-a-number discipline applied to every dial the certificates touch.

  2. Location is symmetry; sign is spectrum. Separating criticality (free, from S₃) from stability (the Hessian sign, the real work) is the single clarifying insight. It immediately tells you which witnesses are cheap (the fixed points) and which carry the whole burden (the eigenvalue signs), and it forbids the common error of citing a symmetry-fixed point as evidence of a minimum.

  3. Decompose the Hessian by the residual symmetry: 3 = 1 ⊕ 2. The S₃ split decouples the volume singlet from the shape doublet exactly, so each can be attacked with the right tool: the doublet by Λ-free flag-manifold curvature, the singlet by the heat-kernel determinant.   4. Isotropy of an irrep Hessian is a structural theorem, not a computation. Because the shape doublet is a single S₃ irrep, its Hessian must be proportional to the identity — so a saddle is structurally impossible, independent of any numerical curvature value. Recognizing this is what turned a banked "−1 saddle" into a diagnosed artifact: the −1 could not be the shape doublet's eigenvalue, so it had to be measuring something else (the volume-contaminated ray at a non-critical point).

  5. The sign is the whole verdict; the magnitude is a distraction. In the shape sector, V = ±R decides MIN vs MAX entirely by sign. The persistent magnitude route-disagreement is non-load-bearing for the stability question — a focusing insight that isolates the one datum worth deriving (the Einstein-frame potential sign).

  6. Collapse the hard fronts onto one object, then firewall it. R1 (the hierarchy via θ_H⋆), R3/R6 (the doublet Casimir magnitude), and R5 (the breathing singlet c_loop) all reduce to one shared object: μ_cell, the spectral value of the uniform operational cell Δ₀. Reducing three problems to one and then proving that one cannot be closed without circularity is the honest endpoint, not a failure.

  7. Faithful-bridge check before grading. The lesson from the retired saddle: a "wall" can be an artifact of the wrong representation.
