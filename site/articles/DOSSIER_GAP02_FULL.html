2. The community gap

2.1 The precise open problem

Pure $SU(3)$ Yang–Mills theory in four spacetime dimensions — the gauge sector of the strong interaction with the quarks removed — is observed to have a mass gap: a strictly positive lowest excitation above the vacuum. This is what makes the strong force short-ranged, the lightest glueball massive rather than massless, and the strong coupling run the way it does. It is an observation-locked fact (short-range nuclear force, massive hadrons, the measured running of $\alpha_s$), not a modeling artifact.

Yet no one has ever proven it. The Clay Millennium Prize problem (Jaffe & Witten, Quantum Yang–Mills Theory, Clay Mathematics Institute, 2000) asks for a mathematically rigorous construction of continuum 4D $SU(N)$ Yang–Mills as a quantum field theory satisfying the Wightman (or Osterwalder–Schrader) axioms, together with a proof that the theory has a positive mass gap $\Delta>0$. The conjunction — existence of the continuum theory and a uniform gap for that same theory — is the prize. The Clay statement itself anticipates that closing it likely requires "important new ideas both in physics and in mathematics."

Stated in the standard constructive-Euclidean arena, the target theorem is:

Target theorem (verbatim, genuinely open). There exists $\Delta>0$ such that $\operatorname{Spec}(H|_{\mathcal H_{\rm phys}})\cap(0,\Delta)=\varnothing$ — there is no sequence of normalized, vacuum-orthogonal, physical states whose energy $\to0$ — for the Hamiltonian $H$ obtained by Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction of a genuine 4D continuum $SU(3)$ pure-gauge measure, with (a) OS reconstruction of that measure, (b) a unique vacuum $\Omega$ with $H\Omega=0$, (c) self-adjoint $H\ge0$, and (d) the gap $\Delta\ge c'\Lambda_{\rm YM}>0$ in physical units.

2.3 Prior attempts and why each falls short

  • Strong-coupling expansion (the "easy route"). At large bare coupling (small $\beta$) the convergent character/cluster expansion gives a rigorous area law and a positive gap in lattice units. But the expansion has a finite radius of convergence in $\beta$ and diverges before $\beta\to\infty$ — its breakdown is associated with the roughening transition. The continuum-limit window $\beta_R\le\beta<\infty$ is precisely the window the strong-coupling argument cannot reach. So strong-coupling confinement, real as it is, does not imply the continuum gap. The hard part is everything between $\beta_R$ and $\infty$ — the fine-lattice / weak-bare-coupling regime where no convergent series controls the gap. (05_uniform_gap_bridge.md §2–3.)
  • Bałaban's RG program. Rigorous block-spin renormalization-group control of the weak-coupling, small-field regime, uniformly in the cutoff — a landmark, genuinely 4D, genuinely non-abelian. But it is a UV-stability program; it controls the small-field end and does not deliver the completed infinite-volume continuum measure with a uniform gap. The large-field / IR end (where confinement and the gap are generated) is not closed by it.
  • Magnen–Rivasseau–Sénéor. A 4D YM phase-space / multiscale expansion constructing the theory with an explicit IR cutoff. The cutoff's removal with a gap bound is exactly the open part.
  • Area-law-to-gap (Route B). Even a continuum area law for the fundamental Wilson loop bounds the static quark–antiquark sector; converting it to a bound on the glueball / physical gauge-invariant spectrum requires the constructed continuum measure and OS reconstruction — an area law is a statement about one observable; the gap is a statement about the whole spectrum of $H$. So this route presupposes most of the bridge rather than bypassing it.
  • Anomaly-matching arguments. A 't Hooft anomaly (e.g. a mixed one-form-center / time-reversal anomaly) constrains the IR but does not by itself force a gap — it can be satisfied by a gapless theory (a conformal or TQFT phase can saturate the anomaly). So even a nonzero anomaly is necessary-not-sufficient.

The structural lesson, verified across machineries: the obstruction is intrinsic to $d=4$ marginality at order-one coupling, not an artifact of any one method.
