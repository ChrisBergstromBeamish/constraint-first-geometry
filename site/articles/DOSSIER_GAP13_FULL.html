2. The community gap

2.1 The precise open problem

Every candidate quantum theory of gravity faces two demands at a black-hole horizon.

  1. Static entropy — the coefficient $1/4$. Bekenstein (1973) and Hawking (1975) established that a black hole carries an entropy $$ S_{\rm BH} = \frac{k_B c^3}{\hbar G}\,\frac{A}{4} = \frac{A}{4 G} \quad(\text{Planck units}), $$ proportional to the horizon area $A$, not its volume, with the locked coefficient $1/4$. The demand is not to quote this number but to derive it: to exhibit the microscopic degrees of freedom whose count $N(A)$ satisfies $\log N(A) = A/4G + (\text{known subleading})$, "for the right reason," with no prefactor tuned to the answer. This is the statistical-mechanical foundation of horizon thermodynamics.

  2. Dynamics — the Page curve. Hawking's semiclassical calculation gives radiation whose entanglement entropy rises monotonically to the full black-hole entropy and beyond, signalling information loss. Page (1993) argued that a unitary evaporation must instead produce radiation entropy that rises and then falls, peaking at the Page time (roughly when half the entropy has radiated) and returning to zero — the Page curve. Reproducing this turnover from first principles is the operational content of "information is not destroyed."

2.2 State of the art

  • Microstate counting. The Strominger–Vafa (1996) count for special supersymmetric extremal black holes in string theory reproduces $A/4G$ exactly from D-brane state degeneracies — a landmark, but restricted to BPS / near-BPS configurations with large supersymmetry, not a realistic 4D Schwarzschild horizon. A universal microstate count for $S = A/4G$ valid across realistic 4D gravity remains unsolved across the whole community.
  • Entanglement-entropy route. A long line of work (Bombelli–Koul–Lee–Sorkin 1986; Srednicki 1993; Susskind–Uglum 1994; Cooperman–Luty 2013) identifies horizon entropy with the vacuum entanglement entropy of quantum fields across the horizon. This route is powerful but carries a notorious scheme-dependence: the leading area term is UV-divergent and is rendered finite by exactly the counterterms that renormalize Newton's constant $G$. As we make rigorous below (§3.3), this makes the coefficient $1/4$ guaranteed by construction — it returns for any matter content, even a free scalar with no microstates — and therefore carries zero independent evidential weight as a derivation.
  • Page curve — the island program. Penington, and Almheiri–Engelhardt–Marolf–Maxfield (2019), together with the replica-wormhole computations of Penington–Shenker–Stanford–Yang and Almheiri–Hartman–Maldacena–Shaghoulian–Tajdini (2020), reproduce the Page curve via the quantum-extremal-surface / island prescription $$ S_{\rm rad} = \min_{\partial I}\ \mathrm{ext}_{\partial I}\left[\frac{A(\partial I)}{4G} + S_{\rm bulk}(R \cup I)\right], $$ where the island $I$ enters after the Page time and forces the turnover. This is the modern state of the art — but it has been demonstrated rigorously only inside special holographic / lower-dimensional setups (JT gravity, doubly-holographic braneworlds), and it presupposes the area term $A/4G$ rather than deriving it. A universal, realistic-4D island construction remains open.
