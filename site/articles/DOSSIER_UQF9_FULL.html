2. The community gap

2.1 The precise open problem

A predictive quantum theory of gravity has to remain sensible at arbitrarily high energy. There are only two defensible exits:

  1. Exhibit a genuine high-energy completion — a truncation-independent, non-Gaussian (interacting) ultraviolet fixed point of the renormalization-group flow at which the dimensionless couplings approach finite values, so that the theory is asymptotically safe and predictive up to infinite energy with finitely many relevant directions; or
  2. Replace "all the way to infinite energy" with something physically defensible — e.g., a principled reason the continuum limit need not be taken at all.

UQF-9 asks whether the frozen Fable geometry survives the ultraviolet by one of these two routes — and, honestly, whether the question is even posed correctly for a theory with a fundamental cost floor.

2.2 History and state of the art

The asymptotic-safety scenario for gravity originates with Weinberg's 1979 proposal that gravity might possess a non-trivial UV fixed point of the Wilsonian flow, with a finite-dimensional critical surface. The modern program is built on the functional / exact renormalization group (the Wetterich equation), and the bulk of the positive evidence comes from truncated flows: one projects the effective average action onto a finite operator basis (Einstein–Hilbert, then \(R^2\), then higher-curvature truncations) and finds an interacting fixed point with an apparently small number of relevant directions. The signature difficulty is that these fixed points are exhibited only at a declared truncation; truncation-independence — that the fixed point and its critical exponents survive as the operator basis is enlarged without bound — is the open object, and it is precisely what no construction in the field has delivered.

State of the art, stated without spin:

  • Best positive bound: interacting fixed points are robust across many truncations with a stable count of relevant directions, which is genuine, suggestive evidence — but it is within-truncation evidence.
  • Best negative signal: as truncations grow, control of the scheme- and gauge-dependence becomes delicate; in the regime relevant here, current FRG evidence trends against the naive truncation route (recorded in this gate's residual ledger as a sharpening of R1, not a refutation).
  • The universal status: exhibiting a truncation-independent non-Gaussian fixed point for quantum gravity is a global open problem of the entire field — unsolved everywhere, not just on this branch. UQF-9 inherits that wall; it does not create it.

2.3 Prior attempts and why each falls short

  • Pure asymptotic safety (truncated FRG). Falls short of closure because truncation-independence is unproved; a fixed point at a chosen truncation is evidence, not a certificate.
  • A literal smallest length (lattice / minimal-length) regulator. This is the classical way to stop infinite descent, but it picks out a preferred frame — the frame in which the length is measured — and therefore breaks Lorentz invariance. It pays the standard, expensive relativity-breaking bill, which is exactly why "spacetime is discrete at the smallest scale" has never been made to sit cleanly with relativity. (the framework's reframe sidesteps this; see §3.3.)
  • Perturbative counterterm bookkeeping. Gravity is perturbatively non-renormalizable; the Seeley–DeWitt counterterm tower \(a_8, a_{10}, \dots\) is unbounded, each order introducing new independent couplings. Treating that tower as a literal physical requirement is exactly the \(a\to 0\) idealization Fable declines as out of scope (the class T-CONT dissolves) — but declining it is a scoping move, not a fixed-point theorem.

The honest position UQF-9 takes is therefore neither "we solved it" nor "we are stuck": it is "we dissolved one well-defined class of the difficulty Lorentz-cleanly, localized the finite remainder to one named refutable object, and we keep the global wall openly on the books."

3.3 The cost-floor reframe (the one genuine non-promoting win)

The reframe is the owner's two-line physics statement:

(L1) Infinite subdivision is harmless. (L2) Infinite prerequisite chains with a nonzero lower cost are impossible. ⟹ Any completable (finite-resource) process has finitely many costly (distinguishable) steps.

The conclusion reframes "the atom." What is quantized is not space — it is cost / action. The smallest irreducible thing is a quantum of cost, not a smallest length. The L1-vs-L2 line is exactly the non-orthogonal / indistinguishable vs orthogonal / distinguishable line: gliding through a continuum of non-orthogonal states is free (no distinguishable milestone is reached); only transitions to orthogonal (distinguishable) states cost, and each such transition costs at least a fixed positive floor. (Source: ROOT_AXIOM_COST_FLOOR_REALIZABILITY.md §0.)

The floor is established physics, in three independent currencies (Source: ROOT_AXIOM_COST_FLOOR_REALIZABILITY.md §1):

  • (a) Margolus–Levitin (1998) — time/action. A system with mean energy \(E\) above its ground state cannot reach an orthogonal (distinguishable) state faster than \[ \tau \ \ge\ \frac{\pi\hbar}{2E}, \] so in time \(T\) it passes through at most \(N_\perp \le 2ET/(\pi\hbar)\) distinguishable transitions — finitely many for finite \(E,T\). (Margolus & Levitin, Physica D 120 (1998) 188.)
  • (b) Landauer — energy/bit. Any logically irreversible bit operation dissipates at least \(\Delta E \ge k_B T \ln 2\), experimentally confirmed (single-bit erasure, 2012 on). (Landauer, IBM J. Res. Dev. 5 (1961) 183; Bérut et al., Nature 483 (2012) 187.)
  • (c) Bekenstein — info/region. A bounded region of radius \(R\) with energy \(E\) holds at most \(S \le 2\pi k_B R E/(\hbar c)\) nats — finite information in a bounded region with finite energy. (Bekenstein, Phys. Rev. D 23 (1981) 287.)

The Lorentz-invariance theorem (T-LI). A length floor breaks Lorentz invariance because length contracts — it picks the frame in which the length is measured. But cost, action, and information are Lorentz scalars: every observer agrees on the action of a process, on whether a bit was erased, on the entropy in an invariantly-defined region. A floor on a scalar selects no preferred frame. The cost-floor reframe therefore delivers the regulating effect of "a smallest something" (an irreducible quantum that stops infinite descent) without paying spatial-granularity's relativity-breaking bill. This is a proved property of the construction (the floored quantity is a scalar), not an additional posit. (Source: ROOT_AXIOM_COST_FLOOR_REALIZABILITY.md §2; COST_FLOOR_WALL_IMPACT_LEDGER.md §0.)

4. The insights we used

These are the moves that produced the progress — now shareable at working-physicist depth, because sharing them is what lets a reader both check and extend the result.

Insight 1 — quantize the scalar, not the length. The deep move is recognizing that the regulating job of a smallest length (stop infinite descent) and its cost (a preferred frame) are separable. Place the floor on cost/action/information — a Lorentz scalar — and you keep the job while dropping the cost. This is why the dissolution is Lorentz-clean: T-LI is not an extra assumption, it is a property of the object being floored. The reframe automatically pays spatial-granularity's biggest bill.

Insight 2 — distinguishability is the right partition. L1 (free subdivision) vs L2 (costed prerequisite) is the non-orthogonal vs orthogonal distinction. The three established floors (Margolus–Levitin, Landauer, Bekenstein) all apply precisely to orthogonal milestones and precisely to nothing in the non-orthogonal continuum. That is the structural reason infinite subdivision is harmless while infinite prerequisite chains are impossible — and the reason the floor is established physics, not a new posit.
