1. Field of the Invention

The disclosed subject matter relates to computer-implemented scientific-computing systems and methods. More particularly, the invention concerns a software platform that receives a structured description of a candidate metric-interface excitation profile — a model data object specifying one or more field-perturbation components of a hypothesized spacetime geometry — subjects that candidate to an ordered sequence of mathematical diagnostic gates, composes the resulting per-gate verdicts under a fail-closed rule into a machine-readable status record called a gate card, and renders a status-labeled, validity-masked visualization of the candidate along a specified vehicle trajectory.

The platform occupies the intersection of three established software disciplines:

  2. Scientific data-validation and compliance tooling — gate-card status engines, fail-closed composition rules, structured verdict data structures, and audit-trail reporting.

2. Background

2.1 The domain problem — speculative metric-interface research and the screening gap

A recurring structural problem in speculative-physics modeling, advanced-concept aerospace studies, and metric-engineering research is that it is trivially easy to write down a spacetime metric and assert that it produces a desirable kinematic effect — for example, an apparent reduction in the proper distance a vehicle must traverse between two points — while silently omitting or deferring the verification of whether:

(a) the apparent effect survives a change of coordinates and is therefore physically real rather than a coordinate artifact produced by a relabeling of the chart;

(b) the metric can be sourced by any admissible, declared distribution of matter and energy — that is, whether there exists a stress-energy tensor, lying within a declared admissible field catalog, that satisfies the Einstein field equations for the proposed metric;

(c) the required source, once reconstructed from the Einstein tensor, violates classical energy conditions and therefore demands exotic matter or negative-energy density that has no observationally confirmed counterpart;

(d) the perturbation is a genuine physical mode rather than a pure-gauge (diffeomorphism) artifact — that is, whether the apparent change in the metric produces any coordinate-invariant curvature;

(e) the candidate's validity depends on unresolved ultraviolet, compactification, or moduli-stabilization assumptions that are open problems in the field itself.

Existing publicly available tools address individual pieces of this analysis in isolation. Computer-algebra systems and numerical relativity libraries — including, by way of example, EinsteinPy, GRTensorIII, SageManifolds, and the xAct suite for Mathematica — compute curvature tensors and invariants from a user-supplied metric. Energy-condition diagnostic utilities evaluate specific energy conditions against a user-supplied stress-energy tensor. Scientific visualization libraries render trajectories and field maps. What no existing tool provides, and what the disclosed platform contributes, is the integration of these fragments into a single ordered, fail-closed pipeline with a machine-checkable gate-card verdict and a constrained, validity-masked visualization.

2.2 The overspecification trap — coordinate artifacts and unsourced metrics

The specific failure mode the platform is designed to prevent is overclaiming by omission. Two technical traps are particularly common in the literature on speculative warp metrics and have concrete, classifiable forms.

The coordinate-artifact trap. A coordinate transformation of a flat Minkowski spacetime — for example, a rescaling x → x' = (1 + f(x)) x — produces an apparent change in the coordinate-measured distance between two points without introducing any genuine spacetime curvature. The Riemann curvature tensor, the Ricci scalar R, and the Kretschmann invariant K = R_{μνρσ}R^{μνρσ} are all identically zero under such a transformation. A screening platform that computes only a proper-distance integral, without verifying that the apparent change is accompanied by a nonzero curvature invariant across multiple coordinate representations, is unable to distinguish a genuine geometric effect from a relabeling. The disclosed platform addresses this gap through a coordinate-invariance diagnostic (Gate F3) that compares the metric target functional across at least two distinct charts and against scalar curvature invariants, and that emits a coordinate-artifact verdict when the apparent change is not accompanied by coordinate-invariant curvature.

The unsourced-metric trap. The Einstein field equations, read in their standard forward direction, take a matter distribution as input and produce a spacetime geometry as output. A speculative warp metric inverts this: it specifies the geometry and then requires the user to exhibit the matter distribution that would source it. The required distribution is not a free parameter; it is uniquely determined by the Einstein tensor of the proposed metric via the algebraic identity T_μν^req = (1/8πG) G_μν. A platform that allows a user to propose a metric and assert in prose that "appropriate matter sources it" without reconstructing T_μν^req and comparing it against a declared available source catalog admits a class of unsourced metrics that carry no physical content. The disclosed platform addresses this through a source-reconstruction module (Gate F4) that applies the Einstein-tensor operator to the candidate profile, exhibits the required stress-energy on a comoving tetrad, and computes a source mismatch norm against any declared source.

2.3 The exoticity wall — classical energy conditions and the shortening-exoticity relationship

A systematic result, known in the general relativity literature through work associated with Tipler, Hawking, and Olum on averaged energy conditions and topological censorship, is that warp-shift metric profiles that produce a genuine, coordinate-invariant forward-path contraction generically require a sourcing stress-energy distribution that violates classical energy conditions. This means the source demands negative energy density or, equivalently, exotic matter — matter whose behavior has no confirmed observational counterpart.

The platform's work, carried through a sequence of diagnostic stages designated S1 (geometry gate), S2 (source and energy gate), and S3 (generation interface gate), reproduces this result for the worked exemplar candidate designated MID-METRIC-001 and for the published warp-metric baselines (Alcubierre class, Natario class, Van Den Broeck class) within the platform's classification pipeline. The key quantitative findings, which constitute enablement evidence rather than claimed physical effects, are:

  • The candidate MID-METRIC-001 (a weak static Gaussian forward-shift lobe with amplitude A = 0.01, Gaussian width σ = 10, support on the interval [30, 70]) carries a genuine, coordinate-invariant forward proper-distance deficit ΔL(30→70) = 0.08837 (analytic estimate ½A√πσ = 0.08862; 0.3% gap), confirmed by nonzero curvature invariants R = −4.0404×10⁻⁴ and K = +8.1624×10⁻⁸, and lying 25× inside the effective-field-theory validity window (χ_max = 0.0404, EFT cutoff ratio < 1).

  • However, the same lobe geometry uniquely determines a required source by the forced-source identity T_μν^req = G_μν/(8πG), which at the lobe center yields an effective energy density ρ_eff = −8.038×10⁻⁶ (negative, i.e., exotic), with null energy condition minimum NEC_min = −1.138×10⁻⁴ (violated), strong energy condition minimum SEC_min = −1.057×10⁻⁴ (violated), and dominant energy condition margin DEC_margin = −1.206×10⁻⁵ (violated).

  • An apex source-matching attempt, designated MID-SRC-001, projecting the required stress-energy against the frozen native-geometry admissible field catalog {δφ, δA_μ^a, δG_AB, δG_μA}, achieves a source closure score of 0.917 (91.7% of the required source matched), with the irreducible residual concentrated in the off-diagonal forward energy-flux component T_{0x}, which is 68% unpaid (ΔT_{0x} = +0.048 against a demand of +0.071).

  • Two capping diagnostics, computed by the platform within its diagnostic sequence, classify the residual at (0,x) — on the model data and within the declared frozen inventory — as structural rather than a tuning limitation (a described limitation of the bounded menu, not a physical no-go theorem): (1) the electromagnetic dominant-energy lock (T_{00} ≥ |T_{0i}| for any Maxwell/Yang-Mills field, with equality only for a null field), which means paying the unpaid forward flux necessarily overshoots the already-matched energy-density channel, moving rather than eliminating the bill; and (2) the static-sourcefree zero-net-flux lock (∇·S = 0 for any static, current-free field, so the net forward Poynting flux through the closed lobe boundary is identically zero), which means a localized sustained-net forward momentum flux would require a real, undeclared matter current absent from the frozen catalog.

These findings are presented as described limitations of the platform's evaluated candidate set and as enablement evidence for the platform's diagnostic capabilities. They are not claimed as propulsion results and do not imply that any candidate is physically realizable as a drive. The platform is not a drive; it is a screening engine, and its most important demonstrated output is a sharpened failure report rather than a passing candidate.

2.4 The locality diagnostic and the prohibition on global transit claims

A further diagnostic internal to the platform's analysis, recorded as Proposition G3-B (No-Global-Aggregation, status TOY_TESTED), classifies a local proper-distance deficit of the type the platform evaluates as carrying no diffeomorphism-invariant global aggregation functional on the model data evaluated. The practical consequence is that a localized forward-shift lobe with compact comoving support produces approximately zero first-order change in the coordinate transit time between two distant points. The local shortening certifies the energetic cost of the lobe geometry; it does not certify a reduction in travel time.

For patent purposes, this result is stated as a described limitation: the platform's status outputs do not include and cannot include any transit-time assertion, Earth-Mars time-reduction claim, or propulsion assertion. Any visualization or report export from the platform that depicted a transit time or a travel-time reduction as a function of the platform's outputs would constitute a claim-boundary violation (exceeding the status ceiling PASS_TOY) and is structurally foreclosed by the gate-card composition rule.

2.5 The ultraviolet-dependency problem and moduli stabilization

A class of candidate metric-interface profiles that survive the geometry and source gates nonetheless requires, for their validity, the resolution of global compactification stability conditions — specifically, the existence of a stabilized moduli potential on a compact extra-dimensional geometry, a controlled decompactification barrier, a calculable and scheme-independent effective vacuum energy density, and the absence of destabilizing light modes. These four sub-conditions, designated collectively as B-UQFC-14-UV-1 in the platform's nomenclature, contain as a sub-problem the cosmological constant problem (why the vacuum energy density takes its observed value rather than the Planck-scale value suggested by naive effective-field-theory estimates). This problem is open in the physics literature generally.

The platform addresses this class of dependence through an ultraviolet-dependency diagnostic (Gate F7) that emits a delegation flag whenever the validity of a candidate, as classified, requires an externally unresolved compactification or ultraviolet condition. The status of the disclosed embodiment's candidate set at this gate is that all candidates whose nominal source closure would be improved by UV-physics assumptions are marked UV_DELEGATED; the assumption B-UQFC-14-UV-1 is designated UNADOPTED and OPEN, and no claim of the platform or of this application asserts or implies its adoption.

3. Prior-Art Landscape

3.1 General-relativity and curvature-tensor software

A number of publicly available software tools compute curvature tensors, scalar invariants, and related geometric quantities from user-supplied metric specifications. Representative examples include EinsteinPy (a Python library for general relativity computations), GRTensorIII (a computer-algebra-system package for tensor computations on curved spacetimes), SageManifolds (a tensor-calculus module for SageMath), and the xAct suite including xTensor and Cadabra. These tools perform forward computations — given a metric, they produce curvature tensors and scalar invariants. They do not perform the inverse operation (reconstructing the required source from the metric), do not compose multiple diagnostic verdicts into a fail-closed gate card, and do not constrain their output visualizations according to a bounded status label. They are components that the disclosed platform orchestrates, not prior art to the pipeline architecture itself.

3.2 Warp-metric literature and simulation codes

A body of published literature, beginning with the 1994 paper by Alcubierre proposing a warp-shift metric, describes specific spacetime geometries designed to produce forward-path contractions. Subsequent contributions by Natario (2002), Van Den Broeck (1999), and others proposed variant geometries with different energy requirements. More recently, White and colleagues at Eagleworks Laboratories proposed modified warp metrics with claimed energy reductions. The software tool Warp Factory (published around 2023) implements a computational pipeline for evaluating warp metric candidates, including energy-condition checking.

The disclosed platform is distinguished from Warp Factory and from the published warp-metric simulation literature on several structural grounds. First, the disclosed platform applies a coordinate-invariance diagnostic that explicitly classifies coordinate artifacts as failing candidates, preventing a relabeling of the chart from being reported as a geometric effect. Warp Factory and the literature generally do not apply this classification gate in the fail-closed pipeline form disclosed here. Second, the disclosed platform reconstructs the required source via the Einstein-tensor identity and computes a quantitative source-mismatch norm against a declared available source catalog, producing a named first-failure field; no existing publicly known tool performs this specific source-audit operation as part of a fail-closed pipeline. Third, the disclosed platform's gate-card data structure, with its named per-gate verdict fields, a first-failure field, an allowed-claim string, and a ceiling-bounded composed status label, is a specific data structure with no known prior-art equivalent. Fourth, the visualization produced by the disclosed platform is coupled to the gate-card status and is rendered with a status watermark and validity mask that prevent it from being interpreted as a certified outcome — a feature not present in prior warp-metric visualization tools.

The Alcubierre, Natario, and Van Den Broeck metric profiles are evaluated by the disclosed platform's pipeline as worked examples. Each passes the coordinate-invariance gate (their curvature is real and nonzero) but fails the source-reconstruction gate with the verdict FAIL_UNSOURCED_METRIC, consistent with the literature's acknowledgment that these metrics require exotic matter. The platform's classification of these profiles is consistent with and independently reproduces the known result. The platform's contribution is not to report this known result for the first time but to supply the computational and data-structure architecture that reports it automatically, fail-closed, with a machine-checkable gate card and a bounded status label, for arbitrary candidate profiles submitted to the pipeline.

3.3 Patent-diligence and compliance-screening software

Scientific and engineering patent-diligence tools exist for classifying the maturity and physical basis of technology claims. Compliance-screening platforms in other engineering domains use structured gate-card or checklist architectures with pass/fail composition rules. The disclosed platform adapts the gate-card architecture to the specific domain of metric-interface and speculative-propulsion candidate screening, combining it with the curvature-tensor computation, source-reconstruction, energy-condition evaluation, gauge classification, and ultraviolet-dependency flagging steps that are specific to the relativistic physics domain. No prior art is known to the applicant that combines all of these elements in an ordered, fail-closed pipeline with a bounded status label for this domain.

4. Summary of the Invention

The disclosed invention is a computer-implemented platform for screening, classifying, projecting, and source-ledger-auditing candidate metric-interface excitation profiles, comprising twelve cooperating invention families arranged as an ordered fail-closed pipeline and a machine-readable gate-card output.

The core inventive concept resides in three specific, non-generic technical elements:

  1. The fail-closed gate-card composition rule. The platform composes per-gate verdicts drawn from an ordered verdict set {FAIL = 0, AUDIT = 1, PASS = 2} by a monotone minimum operation bounded by a predefined ceiling below any physical-claim status label: status = LADDER[min(min_i v_i, CEILING)]. This rule has the formal property that no passing gate can buy back a failing gate, and the composed status can never exceed the ceiling regardless of the number of passing gates. The gate-card data structure records each per-gate verdict, a first-failure field naming the earliest failing gate, and a strongest-allowed-claim field, making the composed verdict auditable and machine-checkable.

  2. The backward source-reconstruction and mismatch-norm computation. The platform applies the Einstein-tensor operator to a candidate metric-interface excitation profile to reconstruct the required stress-energy distribution — the unique distribution that the Einstein field equations demand for the proposed geometry — and computes a quantitative mismatch norm between this required distribution and any declared available source model. This backward operation (geometry to source) is the inverse of the standard forward computation (source to geometry) performed by existing tools, and it constitutes the platform's primary mechanism for detecting the unsourced-metric failure mode.

  3. The status-coupled, validity-masked visualization. The platform renders trajectory and lobe visualizations whose display is coupled to and gated by the gate-card status output, such that a candidate profile failing the coordinate-invariance gate or source gate is prevented from propagating to a certified visualization. The visualization carries a status watermark derived from the composed status label, and regions of the visualization are masked or de-emphasized in proportion to the validity weight of their locally associated verdicts.

The twelve invention families that implement these core concepts are:

  • F1 — Metric target functionals: definition and computation of a forward proper-distance functional over a worldtube region of interest.
  • F2 — Vehicle-comoving lobe definitions: a region operator selecting a front/back-asymmetric subregion ("forward lobe") of the worldtube.
  • F3 — Invariant-distance classifiers: diagnostics comparing the target functional across coordinate charts and against scalar curvature invariants to distinguish coordinate artifacts from coordinate-invariant changes.
  • F4 — Source-reconstruction engines: application of the Einstein-tensor operator to recover the required stress-energy ledger and comparison against a declared source model.
  • F5 — Energy-condition and exoticity engines: evaluation of classical energy conditions on the reconstructed source and computation of a scalar exoticity score.
  • F6 — Gauge and BRST classifiers: discrimination of physical perturbation modes from pure-gauge diffeomorphism artifacts, including application of a BRST physical-state condition.
  • F7 — Ultraviolet-dependency classifiers: detection and flagging of dependence on unresolved compactification or ultraviolet conditions, with issuance of a delegation flag when applicable.
  • F8 — Gate-card validators: composition of the per-gate verdicts under the fail-closed rule into the gate-card data structure and a ceiling-bounded status label.
  • F9 — Mission validity overlays: rendering of trajectory-and-lobe visualizations masked and watermarked according to gate status.
  • F10 — Candidate-mode libraries: parameterized, provenance-bearing catalogs of named candidate profiles with stored prior verdicts.
  • F11 — Optimization and control representations: gate-supervised search representations that evaluate a feasibility gate before admitting a candidate for full pipeline evaluation.
  • F12 — Report and figure exporters: reproducible export of manuscript text, figures, and patent-support material with embedded gate-card annotations and status captions.

The platform is designed so that its utility does not depend on any candidate ever passing. A world in which every candidate in the library fails at least one gate is precisely the world in which the platform's correct-classification function is most demonstrably useful, because each correct rejection is a prevented overclaim. The claims accordingly cover the classification pipeline and its outputs for all inputs — including inputs on which every gate returns a failing verdict — and the platform's output for a failing candidate (a gate card with a named first failure and a defined failing status label) is as well-defined and as valuable as its output for a passing candidate.

19.4 The three detectors — no-new-primitive, no-double-count, chamber-abuse

Operating on the layer-assigned candidate, the family runs three deterministic detectors. Each is a fail-closed test: an unmatched, doubled, or off-chamber component forces a downgrade; it can never raise the status.

(a) The no-new-primitive detector (FAIL_NEW_PRIMITIVE). This detector scans every declared component and confirms it maps, under π, to an enumerated catalog origin. A component that maps to no catalog direction — a new primitive the candidate has introduced beyond the frozen admissible span — is rejected with the token FAIL_NEW_PRIMITIVE. The most important instance the detector defends against is the undeclared forward matter current: a candidate that, in order to supply a missing off-diagonal momentum flux, silently introduces a matter/charge current absent from the catalog. The detector treats the introduction of such a current as a new primitive and fails closed, because declaring a source in prose does not make the source admissible — the source contract is a mathematical membership test against a fixed catalog, not a design choice. In the worked corpus this is the fabrication trap, and it is defused here: the one component the catalog provably cannot supply is exactly the off-diagonal forward flux, and any attempt to supply it by declaring an out-of-catalog current fires FAIL_NEW_PRIMITIVE.

(b) The no-double-count detector (FAIL_DOUBLE_COUNT). This detector confirms that no contribution is entered into the effective source ledger more than once. A common silent error is to attribute the same physical stress-energy contribution to two distinct declared channels — for example, double-entering a single gauge-sector contribution so that its energy density appears inflated. The detector compares the ledger's accumulated contributions against the layer-assigned component set and flags any contribution whose magnitude is consistent with a doubled entry. In a reference negative control, a doubled gauge-sector contribution inflates an energy-density component by a factor of two (a reference value of 3.2×10⁻³ against a correct 1.6×10⁻³); the detector flags FAIL_DOUBLE_COUNT and the deduplicated, correct contribution is handed back to the source and energy gates. A subtle and important property recorded by the family is that removing a phantom double-count subtracts a fake credit and adds no real one: deduplicating an inflated channel makes the ledger honest but does not improve the candidate's standing, because the inflated credit was never real. The detector therefore cannot, by construction, be used to manufacture source payment.

(c) The chamber-abuse detector. This detector confirms that each ⊕-layer (admissibility/projection/ chamber) component is projected onto an allowed region — an admissible "chamber" — of the field space, and flags any component projected onto a forbidden region. The chamber-admissibility projector is the operator that enforces where and under what conditions a perturbation is admissible (§3.1). A component that satisfies the catalog membership test but is projected onto a disallowed chamber is a chamber abuse: it is formally in-catalog yet placed where the catalog forbids it. The detector fails closed on such a component. This is the detector that, in particular, prevents a candidate from "parking" a contribution in a region of field space chosen to evade an upstream gate while nominally remaining in-catalog.

1. Architectural overview — what the pipeline is and what it guarantees

1.1 The pipeline in one sentence

The platform is an importable software package that receives model data describing a candidate metric-interface profile and a vehicle trajectory, routes that model data through an ordered series of mathematical diagnostics arranged as ten composition gates, composes the per-gate verdicts under a fail-closed rule into a single machine-readable gate-card data structure bearing a bounded status label, and emits the gate card together with a validity-masked, watermarked visualization — the composed status label overriding, in every case, whatever standing the candidate's own configuration declared for itself.

1.2 The two architectural guarantees

The architecture is engineered to deliver two guarantees that hold for every candidate, on every input, by construction rather than by discipline:

  1. Status-meet override of self-report. No candidate can declare its own standing. A candidate's configuration may admit what it has not done (an honesty field), but the configuration schema contains no operational pass value, so a candidate cannot assert a status it has not earned. The composed status label is computed solely by the gate-card validator from the per-gate verdicts; it is the meet (the greatest-lower-bound, realized as a monotone minimum) of the contributing verdicts under a bounded ceiling. Where a candidate's self-declared floor and the validator's computed status disagree, the validator's status governs and the self-report is recorded only as an audited input, never as an output.

  2. Fail-closed monotonicity with a hard ceiling. The composed status is provably no stronger than the weakest contributing gate and provably no stronger than a fixed ceiling below any physical-claim label. There exists no input vector of verdicts for which the platform emits a status above the ceiling or above its weakest gate, and there exists no path by which a passing gate "buys back" a failed gate. This is the data-structure-and-rule expression of the platform's governing maxim, downgrade, do not invent.

These two guarantees are what make the platform's output trustworthy by construction. They are recited in the independent claims (the fail-closed composition and the status-meet override) and refined in the dependent claims; the present part of the specification supplies the corresponding structure and algorithms so that those claims are enabled under 35 U.S.C. §112.

1.3 Package layout and the sibling-gate-infrastructure rule

In a representative embodiment the platform is organized as a software package with cooperating subpackages: a geometry/tensor engine; a metric-target-functional and forward-lobe module; an invariant-distance classifier; a source-reconstruction-and-billing engine; an energy-condition / exoticity engine; a gauge / BRST classifier; a native-origin classifier and admissibility/chamber projector; an ultraviolet-sensitivity router; a gate-card validator; a mission-visualization module; a candidate-mode library; a gate-supervised optimizer; and a report exporter. A gate-infrastructure subpackage — carrying the configuration schema, the gate-card schema, the closure-ladder tokens, the downgrade-token table, and the forbidden-phrase scanner — is a sibling of (not a child of) every diagnostic subpackage. The sibling layout is load-bearing: the gate infrastructure must be importable by every engine and must exist before any geometry module compiles its first result into a status, so placing it under any one diagnostic subpackage would permit a future module to import a diagnostic without importing the gates. A single-source convention object binding signature, sign, and index-order conventions is loaded once and read by every module, and a self-test asserts that no module hard-codes a sign — the structural remedy for the most common silent error in a hand-rolled tensor engine.

A complementary build-order rule makes un-validated infrastructure unable to emit a status: the gate infrastructure and a validated tensor engine (which must first pass a suite of known-metric self-tests) are constructed before any candidate-evaluation step, so that a candidate cannot even be evaluated by un-built or un-validated code and therefore cannot have its status float upward through an un-gated path.

2.2 The fail-closed composition and status-meet override

Each gate verdict is assigned an integer level over the ordered verdict set, in a representative embodiment v_i ∈ {FAIL = 0, AUDIT = 1, PASS_TOY = 2}, augmented by the routing values UV_DELEGATED and FORBIDDEN_PROMOTION which map into the failing/audit band (a FORBIDDEN_PROMOTION is a hard fail; a UV_DELEGATED is no stronger than an audit value). A claim-boundary verdict computed by the forbidden-phrase scanner participates as one further contributing verdict. The composed status level and label are

status_level = min_i v_i                                    # the meet: weakest gate dominates
status_label = LADDER[ min(status_level, CEILING) ],   CEILING = PASS_TOY

so that for every verdict vector v the composed status satisfies both status(v) ≤ min_i v_i and status(v) ≤ CEILING, with no exception path by which a passing verdict raises the composed status. The closure ladder is the ordered set 0 UNDEFINED → 1 TOY_DEFINED → 2 TOY_TESTED → 3 EFT_CANDIDATE → 4 PHYSICS_AUDIT_READY → 5 PHYSICAL_CLAIM, and the ceiling PASS_TOY is a toy/simulation status below EFT_CANDIDATE; level 5 (PHYSICAL_CLAIM) is out of scope and structurally unreachable.

The status-meet override of self-report is a property of this same composition. The candidate's configuration carries a claimed status floor that, at configuration time, may not exceed an audit/toy value; promotion above that floor is reserved to the validator. The validator ignores the claimed floor when computing the composed status — it composes only the per-gate verdicts — and then records the claimed floor alongside the computed status purely as an audited input. If a candidate's configuration were to (improperly) carry a stronger self-declared standing than its gates earn, the meet composition discards it: status_label is a function of the verdict vector alone, so a candidate cannot raise its own status by declaring it. The schema's omission of any operational pass value is the first line of this defense; the meet composition is the second; and the forbidden-phrase scanner's claim-boundary verdict (which fails the composition if the candidate's strongest-allowed-claim string contains a forbidden physical assertion) is the third.

2.3 Why a passing gate cannot buy back a failed gate

Because status_level is a minimum, the contribution of any passing gate to the composed status is bounded above by the minimum already set by the weakest gate; a passing value can only fail to lower the status, never raise it. Formally there is no function from the verdict vector to the status that is both consistent with the recited composition and admits a buy-back, because min is monotone non-decreasing in each argument and is dominated by its least argument. The architecture therefore admits, by construction, none of the two subtle defects a reviewer would probe for: a candidate cannot be promoted by a single favorable metric (the energy gate's clean-pass exclusion and the meet composition forbid it), and a failed gate cannot be offset by a passing gate (the minimum forbids it).

3. The gate-card schema

3.1 The gate-card as the platform's unit of output

The gate card is the platform's fundamental output object and the locus of its most defensible novelty: it is a concrete, machine-readable data structure that records, for a candidate, every per-gate verdict together with the composed status, the named first failure, and an explicit allowed/forbidden-claims pair, so that the verdict is auditable, reproducible, and incapable of expressing a stronger claim than the gates earned. In one embodiment the gate card is a JSON object; the schema is enforced by a validator that refuses to emit a card lacking a status or a named first failure.

3.2 The seven schema fields

A representative gate-card schema carries the following named fields. The seven fields the present architecture emphasizes are geometry_origin, layer_assignment, projection_path, source_billing, first_failure, strongest_allowed_claim, and patent_residue; the per-gate verdict sub-records and provenance fields accompany them.

  • geometry_origin — the native-origin classification of each field-perturbation component: for each component, the internal-geometry factor of the frozen-background inventory it is found to inhabit (or an out-of-catalogue value), recorded as a provenance label. In the worked exemplar the inventory enumerates a fixed finite catalogue of admissible native directions partitioned among the internal-geometry factors; a component lying outside the catalogue sets geometry_origin to an undeclared-primitive value and forces the native-origin verdict to a failing value. This field records where the candidate's content comes from, and it is the field that distinguishes a genuine native-origin component from a smuggled out-of-catalogue field.

  • layer_assignment — the ×/⊕/⊗ layer to which each component is mapped: the metric/geometric base layer (×), the admissibility/projection/chamber layer (⊕), or the bundle/operator/quantization layer (⊗). The field records, per component, the assigned layer and whether the assignment is consistent with the component's geometry_origin. A component that is unmapped, multiply-mapped, or mapped inconsistently with its provenance forces the layer verdict to a failing value. This field records which discipline gates apply to each component.

  • projection_path — the sequence of admissibility/chamber projectors applied to the candidate before any downstream geometric quantity is read, in a representative embodiment the composition Π_adm = Π_freeze · Π_chamber · Π_layer · Π_dup of a freeze projector, a chamber-membership projector, a layer projector, and a deduplication projector, together with the admissibility residual. The field records that the candidate was projected onto the admissible native subspace and by what path, and it carries the necessary-but-not-sufficient flag: an empty admissibility residual is necessary for a passing status but does not by itself raise the status.

This field records the accounting of what would be required to source the candidate and how much of it the frozen inventory can account for — never an assertion that the source physically exists.

  • first_failure — the named earliest-failing gate in reading order, the candidate's terminal characterization. The field is mandatory: the validator refuses to emit a card without it. In the worked exemplar first_failure = "source_check (HC-02): no declared stress-energy ledger / closure_score = 0.917 < 1, residual concentrated at (0,x)".

4. The downgrade tree

4.1 First-firing-rule semantics

The gate-card validator applies an ordered rule table in which the first firing rule sets the terminal status — realizing the "name the first failure" mandate — and in which no rule promotes. The table is fail-closed at its root: a schema-invalid configuration or card, or a model-identifier mismatch between configuration and card, fails immediately; thereafter the rules fire in gate reading order, each mapping a gate failure to a named downgrade token and a bounded status. A representative rule table:

RULE 0  schema_valid(config) AND schema_valid(card) AND model_id_match
        else -> FAIL  ("malformed or mismatched artifact")
RULE 1  card.gates.claim_boundary == FAIL  -> FORBIDDEN_PROMOTION
RULE 2  card.gates.invariant_distance == FAIL -> FAIL_COORDINATE_ONLY
RULE 3  card.gates.source == FAIL -> FAIL_UNSOURCED_METRIC
        AND assert "propulsion" NOT in card.strongest_allowed_claim
RULE 4  card.gates.energy == FAIL -> FAIL_HIDDEN_EXOTICITY
        NOTE energy gate may NEVER be PASS_TOY; AUDIT is its ceiling
RULE 5  card.gates.causality == FAIL -> FAIL_CAUSALITY      (veto-only)
RULE 6  card.gates.gauge == FAIL -> FAIL_PURE_GAUGE
RULE 7  card.gates.admissibility == FAIL -> FAIL_NEW_PRIMITIVE | FAIL_DOUBLE_COUNT
RULE 8  card.gates.uv == UV_DELEGATED -> UV_DELEGATED
        AND assert "global stability solved" NOT in card.strongest_allowed_claim
RULE 9  card.gates.mission lacks status OR is global-Δt -> FAIL_MISSION_OVERCLAIM
DEFAULT  status <- min_i v_i, bounded by CEILING = PASS_TOY

4.2 The downgrade-token enumeration

The downgrade token recorded in the gate card is selected according to the first failing diagnostic and is drawn from a fixed enumeration, each token naming a specific failure mode: FAIL_COORDINATE_ONLY (the apparent deficit is a coordinate artifact), FAIL_UNSOURCED_METRIC / AUDIT_SOURCE_MISMATCH (the required source is not accounted for by the frozen inventory within tolerance), FAIL_HIDDEN_EXOTICITY / TOY_MODEL_EXOTIC (the required source violates an energy condition), FAIL_CAUSALITY (a chronology-violation indicator), FAIL_PURE_GAUGE (the perturbation is diffeomorphism- or gauge-exact), AUDIT_QUANTUM (the interacting quantized sector is not BRST-confirmed), FAIL_NEW_PRIMITIVE (an out-of-catalogue field, or a declared absent current, was introduced), FAIL_DOUBLE_COUNT (a single native-origin contribution was billed more than once), UV_DELEGATED (validity is delegated to an unresolved compactification or UV condition), FORBIDDEN_PROMOTION (a route would edit a frozen modulus, record the named open assumption as adopted, or assert a forbidden physical claim), and FAIL_MISSION_OVERCLAIM (a local deficit was aggregated into a global travel-time claim, or a plot was rendered without a status mask). The enumeration is closed: every failure resolves to exactly one first-firing token, so the terminal characterization is deterministic.

4.3 The forbidden-promotion subtree (self-adoption is structurally unreachable)

A distinguished subtree of the downgrade tree handles attempts at self-promotion. Any operation that would (i) edit a member of the read-only generating-moduli set (the frozen lobe amplitude A, width σ, support, or declared field content), (ii) declare an absent matter or charge current to close the off-diagonal residual, or (iii) record the named open assumption B-UQFC-14-UV-1 as adopted, is routed to FORBIDDEN_PROMOTION and fails the composition. The named open assumption is held in a read-only status that an automated module may read as UNADOPTED but is structurally prohibited from writing as adopted; adoption is reserved to an authorized human/attorney act outside the automated pipeline. This is the architectural mechanism by which the platform cannot, even in principle, promote a candidate past the barrier by tampering with the frozen inputs or by adopting the open physics on its own authority.

5. The validity-weighted mission overlay (watermarked)

5.1 Render-enable coupled to status

The mission-visualization module's render-enable is coupled to and gated by the gate-card's composed status: the module cannot render an overlay for a candidate whose gate card lacks a status or whose composed status is below the render threshold, and any overlay it does render is masked and watermarked according to the status label. This coupling — render-enable gated by a status data structure — is the architectural feature that changes the behavior of the system (an invalid candidate's overlay is never produced) rather than merely displaying a result.

5.2 Validity-weighting and masking

The overlay is validity-weighted: a validity weight is computed as a monotone function of the contributing verdicts, lowest when any verdict is a failing value, and the visual emphasis of each region of the depicted forward lobe is scaled by the validity weight. A region whose locally evaluated verdicts include a failing value is de-emphasized in proportion to the failing value; in a representative embodiment the benefit field collapses to zero wherever any gate failed, so that no portion of the overlay is rendered as a certified outcome when the status is below the ceiling. Where the depiction includes a planetary-transit or trajectory-to-destination element, that element is rendered only in association with the status label and a watermark and is masked so that it does not assert a physical transit time. The locality diagnostic feeds this masking: because the local proper-distance deficit carries no diffeomorphism-invariant global-aggregation functional (the locality_flag is LOCAL), any attempt to aggregate the local deficit into a global travel-time quantity is downgraded to FAIL_MISSION_OVERCLAIM, and the overlay records that an invariant deficit certifies a cost and not a capability.

5.3 The watermark

Every rendered overlay carries a status watermark stamping the composed status label and indicating that the overlay is a simulation-gated result. An unwatermarked or unmasked plot does not exist as an output of the platform: the report exporter refuses to write a figure whose caption is not generated from the gate card and whose status and first-failure are not embedded. The watermark is therefore not decoration but a structural property of the output — the visual analogue of the gate card's status field.

6. The cross-gate prune (monotone, pure-function composition)

The ten gates do not run as ten independent analyses; they run as one ordered prune that collapses monotonically.
