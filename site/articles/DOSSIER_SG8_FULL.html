2. The community gap

2.1 The precise open problem

Flavor is the Standard Model's deepest unsolved input. The SM has 19 free parameters; the majority of them live in the flavor sector: nine charged-fermion masses (or equivalently nine Yukawa couplings spanning roughly six orders of magnitude, from the electron at ~0.5 MeV to the top at ~173 GeV), three CKM mixing angles, one CKM CP-violating phase, and — once neutrino mass is included — three more mixing angles, at least one Dirac CP phase, and the neutrino mass-squared splittings. Every one of these is measured and typed in by hand. No accepted theory derives the pattern: the masses, the mixings, the hierarchy, and the CP phase are simply free inputs.

The structure that cries out for explanation is striking and specific:

  • Hierarchy. Within each charged sector the masses fall in a steep geometric-looking cascade. Empirically $m_t/m_c \approx m_c/m_u$ and $m_b/m_s \approx m_s/m_d$ — the same ratio recurs between consecutive generations, hinting at a single underlying step rather than three independent numbers per sector.
  • Mixing smallness and hierarchy. The CKM matrix is nearly diagonal, with off-diagonal magnitudes organized in powers of a small parameter ($\lambda \approx 0.22$, the Wolfenstein expansion). The PMNS matrix, by contrast, is anarchic — large mixing angles. Any theory of flavor must produce both patterns from the same machinery.
  • CP violation. There is exactly one physical CP phase in the quark sector (the Jarlskog invariant $J_{\rm CKM} \approx 3\times10^{-5}$ is tiny but nonzero), and the measured CKM phase sits near $\delta_{\rm CKM} \approx 65.5°$ (PDG, in the standard Wolfenstein-aligned convention).

2.2 State of the art and prior attempts

The textbook landscape of flavor model-building, and why each line falls short of a derivation:

  • Froggatt–Nielsen / abelian flavor symmetries. A spontaneously broken $U(1)$ flavor charge assigns each fermion an integer charge; mass-matrix entries scale as $(\langle\phi\rangle/M)^{n}$, generating hierarchy from a single small ratio. Shortfall: the charges are chosen to fit the observed pattern, and the order-one coefficients in front of each power are unconstrained — the mechanism reproduces the shape but never removes the freedom; it relocates ~the same number of inputs into charges + coefficients.
  • Discrete non-abelian flavor symmetries ($A_4$, $S_4$, $\Delta(27)$, modular flavor symmetry). These can predict specific mixing patterns (e.g. tri-bimaximal PMNS) and, in modular-symmetry constructions, make Yukawa couplings modular forms of a single complex modulus $\tau$. Shortfall: the modulus $\tau$ is typically a free VEV chosen to fit the data, the matter assignments are model choices, and the constructions usually predict the leptonic sector well while leaving the quark hierarchy comparatively unconstrained.
  • Texture / zero ansätze (Fritzsch and descendants). Imposing zeros in the Yukawa matrices yields relations like $\lvert V_{us}\rvert \approx \sqrt{m_d/m_s}$. Shortfall: the textures are imposed, not derived, and the most economical ones are now excluded by precision data.
  • Anarchy (for neutrinos). Treating PMNS entries as order-one random numbers explains the large leptonic mixing statistically. Shortfall: it is an explicit abdication of prediction — it explains why mixing is large but predicts no specific value.

The common thread: every accepted approach reorganizes the flavor freedom (into charges, into a fitted modulus, into textures, into priors) but none eliminates it down to a small structural input set with the per-entry freedom genuinely removed. That is the gap. The contribution of SG-8 is to make the within-sector ratios and all mixings/phases forced functions of a small frozen structure — with the honest concession that the absolute scales remain calibrated.
