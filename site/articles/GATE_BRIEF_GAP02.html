Gap-02 — The Yang-Mills Mass Gap

What this gate must establish

This gate must show that the strong force has a positive mass gap — a strictly nonzero lowest excitation above the vacuum. This is exactly the Yang-Mills mass gap of the Clay Mathematics Institute's Millennium Prize Problems. The gap is what makes the strong force short-ranged and the lightest glueball massive rather than massless; it is observed — the short-range nuclear force, massive hadrons, and the measured running of the strong coupling all record it — so this is an observation-locked obligation, not an artifact. The question the gate answers is whether the framework meets that obligation honestly, and what part of it the framework owns.

The geometry's role (load-bearing, not validation)

The frozen 13D geometry fixes where the gap's origin comes from — SU(3) emerges from the shape (K₆ = SU(3)/T²) — and it pins the boundary data: the strong-force scale Λ_YM is set by dimensional transmutation from α₃(M_Z) and the unification scale, the unit every gap statement is measured against, not a tuned number. The 13D-specific structure it supplies (the coset color source, the KK tower, the spin-c index, the internal-circle fold) is UV-origin and admissibility discipline: it settles where the strong force comes from and why a gap must exist. It does not, by itself, supply an infrared proof lever — by Wilsonian universality the IR confining dynamics is indifferent to the UV completion. That honest limitation is exactly what the granularity axiom below is for: it is what lets the gate close on its own terms rather than on a continuum proof the framework neither owns nor needs.
