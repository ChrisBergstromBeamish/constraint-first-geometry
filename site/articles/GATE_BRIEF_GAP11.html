Gap-11 — Dark Matter

What this gate must establish

This gate must show that the frozen 13D geometry supplies a viable dark-matter candidate — a stable, neutral state with the right relic abundance — without that candidate being fitted to the observed cosmological data. It must produce a particle whose stability and portal to ordinary matter follow from the geometry's structure and a genuinely calculated coupling, then compare the resulting abundance to the measured Ω_DM h² ≈ 0.12 only after the prediction is frozen.

The geometry's role (load-bearing, not validation)

The candidate is not invented for the purpose. The folded hypercharge circle S¹_Y/ℤ₂ — a small looped-up extra direction tied to hypercharge — localizes a ℤ₂-odd, zero-hypercharge state at a fixed point, and that same boundary structure is what makes it stable: the ℤ₂-odd parity forbids decay, and the zero hypercharge keeps the coupling renormalizable. In the corpus's selection audit this state was picked out 17 of 18 times across five independent reweightings — a structural selection, not a fit to any abundance. The geometry carries the candidate's identity, its two independent stability legs, and the fixed inventory of allowed interactions its portal must be drawn from. Nothing invented is allowed in: every coupling the particle has must be one of the named, already-accounted-for operators from that inventory, and the portal strength must be a real overlap integral rather than an asserted value.
