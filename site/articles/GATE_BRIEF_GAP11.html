Gap-11 — Dark Matter

What this gate must establish

This gate must show that the frozen 13D geometry supplies a viable dark-matter candidate — a stable, neutral state with the right relic abundance — without that candidate being fitted to the observed cosmological data. Concretely, it must produce a particle whose stability, production rate, and present-day abundance follow from the geometry's structure and a derived coupling, then compare the resulting abundance to the measured Ω_DM h² ≈ 0.12 only after the prediction is frozen.

The geometry's role (load-bearing, not validation)

The candidate is not invented for the purpose: the boundary structure of the geometry's S¹_Y/ℤ₂ factor localizes a ℤ₂-odd, zero-hypercharge state at a fixed point, and that same structure is what makes it stable (the ℤ₂-odd parity forbids decay; zero hypercharge keeps the coupling renormalizable). In the corpus's selection audit this state was picked out 17 of 18 times across five reweightings — a structural selection, not a fit to any abundance. Two viability gates are already banked: renormalizable stability (Y = 0) and a one-sided KK-overclosure pass across the inflaton band. The geometry thus carries the candidate's identity and stability; it does not, on its own, fix the one number the relic abundance depends on.

Current honest status

Status: OPEN (recorded in the corpus as "conditional, blocked on a named gate"). This is a serious candidate / partial unification — not validated. Promotions: 0.

The single blocker is the portal normalization N_portal: the derived coefficient of the operator coupling the candidate to the Standard-Model bath, which must come from a wavefunction-overlap integral on S¹_Y/ℤ₂ and not be tuned to reproduce 0.12. Until it is derived, neither the freeze-in relic integral (Ω_DM h²) nor the spin-independent cross-section σ_SI can be computed. The blocking objects are:

  • The portal operator identity — the explicit coupling (Higgs-portal-like, Wilson-line/KK-mixing, or higher-dimension) is not yet named in the corpus, and its ℤ₂ charge must be checked against the stability gate.
  • The portal normalization N_portal — a finite, compact geometric overlap integral; specifiable but unrun, and an owner/machine computation rather than a logical argument. An open ambiguity (whether the frozen λ_p(0.12) ~ 10⁻¹¹ is this normalization) could narrow the block but is unresolved.
  • A frozen reheating temperature T_RH — inherited-conditional from Gap-09 (reheating), currently a ~3-orders-of-magnitude band, itself cascading on Gaps 08 and 04.

A null-forecast is banked at forecast grade: σ_SI ≈ 10⁻⁵⁵–10⁻⁶⁰ cm², roughly 6–11 orders of magnitude below the neutrino floor. The falsifier is therefore one-sided — no null experiment can confirm the candidate; only a positive WIMP-like signal can adjudicate, and any such signal would kill the minimal candidate.

What would close it

There is exactly one new derivation this gate needs of its own: evaluate the portal-normalization overlap integral on S¹_Y/ℤ₂ (after naming the portal operator), then run the standard freeze-in Boltzmann quadrature for Ω_DM h² and compute σ_SI from the same coefficient — comparing to 0.12 only after the prediction is frozen. This requires a frozen Gap-09 T_RH band as input, so closure here is gated on Gap-09 (and transitively Gaps 08/04). Because the load-bearing adjudication is an experiment with a null-forecast far below current reach, the honest endpoint is asymmetric: the candidate can be refuted by a direct-detection signal but cannot be confirmed by any foreseeable null result.

What this gate reduces to — and its honest status

Status: OPEN / conditional, blocked on a named gate. Serious, structurally selected candidate — not validated. No number is newly derived and nothing is upgraded.

What the gate reduces to

  • Measured anchor. The only number the gate compares against is the observed dark-matter abundance \(\Omega_{\rm DM}h^2 \approx 0.12\), used strictly as a comparison target for a frozen prediction — never fitted to.
  • The candidate state. A stable, electrically neutral, parity-odd state pinned to a fixed point of the compact \(S^1_Y/\mathbb{Z}_2\) factor, forced out by the geometry's boundary structure. Its parity forbids decay and its zero hypercharge keeps the coupling well-behaved; the same structure that localizes it makes it stable. It is selected 17 of 18 times across five independent reweightings — carried at candidate grade, not a certified result and not a fit to any abundance.
  • The coupling coefficient. The abundance turns on one geometry-defined coefficient — the normalization of the operator coupling the candidate to ordinary matter, a finite overlap integral on the boundary geometry. This coefficient has not been computed.
  • Inherited input. The relic-abundance calculation also consumes a reheating temperature from the (still open) reheating gate, so closure here cannot outrun that upstream input.

Honest endpoint

What is established, given the observed inputs: the frozen geometry genuinely supplies and stabilizes a structurally selected dark-matter candidate (its identity and stability), and a one-sided over-closure check is banked. What stays precisely open: the single geometry-defined coupling normalization has not been computed; the prior question of which operator (if any) actually governs the coupling — and whether the abundance is even set this way — must be resolved first; and the reheating-temperature input is inherited from another open gate. The decisive test is asymmetric and experimental: the forecast direct-detection cross-section sits roughly 6–11 orders of magnitude below the neutrino floor, so a positive signal would refute the minimal candidate, but no null result can confirm it. The only path to a derived result is to settle the operator question target-blind, then compute the coupling normalization and the abundance on a frozen reheating band — comparing to 0.12 only after the prediction is frozen.
