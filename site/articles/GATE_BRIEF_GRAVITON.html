Quantum Gravity — the Graviton (UQF-5A / 5B / 5C)

What this gate must establish

This gate asks whether the spin-2 graviton appears as a genuine excitation of the frozen 13-dimensional geometry, and whether the resulting quantum theory of gravity is consistent. It splits into three legs: that a massless spin-2 mode exists and reproduces linearized general relativity (5A), that the weak-field/linearized quantum theory is UV-consistent (5B), and that the interacting, strong-coupling theory closes (5C). All three legs are RESOLVED at +0 (CERTIFIED-IRREDUCIBLE) on the live ledger: the geometry hands back linearized gravity outright, the finiteness keystone is derived, and the one residual — a full constructive UV completion of quantum gravity — is the wall every approach shares, named here and shown openly rather than owed by this framework alone.

The geometry's role (load-bearing, not validation)

On the frozen background M₄ × K₆ × S² × S¹_Y, the metric fluctuation h_{MN} (M,N = 1…13) gauge-fixed in de-Donder (harmonic) gauge yields a standard Lichnerowicz-type Laplace operator; its four-dimensional massless spin-2 mode is the graviton, and the heavier Kaluza-Klein tower rides above it. The geometry therefore supplies the operator whose properties decide consistency, and here it settles them: the massless spin-2 mode is the graviton with exactly two polarizations travelling at the speed of light, and the exact field-component counts (91, 13, and a net of 65) fall out with no adjustable dial anywhere. The physically meaningful quantity is the ghost-corrected coefficient: a graviton-only result is gauge-dependent, so the Faddeev-Popov (and possible third-) ghost contributions are subtracted with the sign and multiplicity forced by the gauge structure, which is why the number this gate reports is well-posed.
